Week 5 Power Rankings

With the large-school races canceled, Divisions 2 and 3 took center stage at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational.

By Ethan Barossi  ·  October 13, 2026

Weston boys celebrating their win in the medium schools race at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational.

With the large-school races at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational canceled, the smaller schools had a chance to shine this weekend. Every movement in this week’s power rankings came from Divisions 2 and 3, giving us a clearer picture of how both divisions could shape up heading into November.

#7 Groton-Dunstable

This one may surprise some people, but based on what we’ve seen, Groton-Dunstable enters the week as our top-ranked Division 2 team. Do we think they’ll still hold that spot in November? No, but we’ll get into that later.

Although Andrew Kosiba didn’t have his best race this weekend, he’s still one of the top individuals to beat in the division. Kosiba leads his squad with a personal best of 15:30. Micah Pierantozzi is their #2 runner on paper, and he delivered a big performance this weekend, running 16:02 to place 11th overall.

The Duane brothers help round out this squad. Ashton Duane ran 16:26 this weekend, while Cameron Duane finished in 16:55.

However, the fastest of the three brothers didn’t race this weekend. Senior Greyson Duane ran 16:19 at the Frank Kelley Invitational last weekend but enters the season with a personal best of 15:36 from his sophomore year.

We’re not going to speculate about why he didn’t race, but we believe that with G. Duane in the lineup and running at his best, Groton-Dunstable could have come away with the team victory by a comfortable margin.

#9 Hopedale

Hopedale secured the victory in the small-school race this weekend, proving they’re the team to beat in Division 3.

Quinn Cook also looks like a serious contender for the individual title after winning the race in a staggering 15:15.

Ben Stone placed 10th overall with a personal best of 16:03, while Connor Fitzgibbon finished 12th in 16:09. Cedric Author (16:40) and Jack Murphy (16:42) round out the team’s top five, with both runners setting their personal bests at the Frank Kelley Invitational last weekend.

Ethan DeWolf provides some serious depth for this squad as well, having run a personal best of 17:10 last weekend.

Hopedale has established itself as one of the strongest Division 3 teams in Massachusetts. The question now is whether it can carry this momentum into November.

#12 Weston

Weston comes in at #12, for now…

This team won the medium-school race at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational, and unlike Groton-Dunstable, Weston was competing without multiple runners at full strength.

Hunter Waechter came away with the individual title, delivering an outstanding performance to upset some of Division 2’s favorites, including Calvin Miller and Andrew Kosiba.

Mathew Fitzgibbon placed 12th overall in 16:04. However, we don’t believe we’ve seen Fitzgibbon at his best yet. His absence from the Bay State Invitational and recent performances suggest he may still be working his way back to full strength.

Fitzgibbon is one of Division 2’s top returners, and when he’s at his best, we expect him to be running alongside Waechter near the front of major races.

Jonah Watkins (16:25) and Thomas Moser (16:33) provide excellent depth for the squad. Keep an eye on Watkins specifically. He’s only a freshman and has already put down an impressive time. Kevin Han rounds out the team’s top five with a 16:56.

But there’s one more important piece of this team that makes Weston even scarier: Aarav Mehta.

Mehta has been working his way back from injury and ran 18:12 in the JV race this weekend at a controlled effort. He enters the season with a personal best of 15:57 and could become a major factor for Weston if he returns to full strength before the postseason.

We’ve placed Weston at #12 for now because of the uncertainty surrounding Fitzgibbon and Mehta. However, we believe this team has the potential to climb significantly higher once all its pieces are in place.

Watch out for the Waechter, Fitzgibbon, and Mehta trio heading into the postseason. With those three leading the way and impressive depth behind them, Weston has its eyes on the Division 2 crown.

#19 Amherst-Pelham

Amherst-Pelham completely surpassed our expectations this weekend, placing second in the medium-school race at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational.

Earlier in the season, we learned that the team was missing one of its top returners, which contributed to our decision to move Amherst-Pelham down the rankings. However, even without him, this squad is proving it can still hold its own against some of the state’s best teams.

Calvin Miller placed third overall in 15:39. Otis Fairey followed with a massive personal best of 16:06, while Peter Nedeau finished third for the team in 16:14.

Nedeau has reportedly dealt with some injury setbacks, highlighted by his absence from the Amherst Invitational, but his recent performance suggests he may be making progress toward returning to his best.

However, Amherst-Pelham’s depth is what really convinced us to move them back into the rankings.

Nicholas Workman was the team’s fourth runner this weekend, finishing in 16:48. He entered the season with a personal best of 17:56, making his improvement particularly impressive.

The same can be said for Alhassan Abdel-Maksoud, who entered the season with a 17:57 personal best but recently ran 17:15 to round out the squad.

These improvements have transformed Amherst-Pelham into a much more competitive team than we originally anticipated.

Watch out for Amherst-Pelham at the Division 2B Championship. We expect them to be battling Northampton for the runner-up trophy.
