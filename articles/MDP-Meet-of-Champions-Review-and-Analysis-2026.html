Meet of Champions Review & Analysis

The 2026 cross-country season has officially come to an end with the biggest meet of the year. With so many special performances, upsets, and unforgettable moments, this meet gave us plenty to talk about. So let's dive right in.

Division 1

Spotlight: Brookline

Well, they did it again. Brookline claimed its 5th straight All-State title, taking a huge win over Cambridge Rindge & Latin.

Coming through about 2.5 miles into the course, Brookline actually sat in 2nd place, but the Warriors weren't going to settle for second. In a gutsy final lap, Nam Danh, who was actually the squad's #6 runner at the time, knew he had to step up for his team. He moved into the #5 spot on the squad, picking off 10 runners in the final half-mile of the course. Nam's incredible performance helped carry the Warriors to their 5th consecutive title.

His teammates also put together some amazing performances. Up front, Liam Hartmann and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem were taking control of the race, competing with the best runners the state had to offer. Their incredible efforts resulted in Hartmann placing 3rd overall and Abdel-Dayem finishing 5th, scoring some extremely valuable low points for the Warriors.

Next, Elias Thomasson and Matt Ritter closed out their seasons with some outstanding performances. Knowing what was at stake, Thomasson put together one of the best individual performances of the day, placing 12th overall. It's hard to believe that just last year, Thomasson was a 16:30 runner, but his commitment to the sport and his teammates truly paid off this season.

Ritter also deserves a ton of credit. We don't talk about him as much as some of the other members of this team, but his performance was just as important. Knowing the team title was on the line, Ritter moved up six spots in the final lap, doing everything he could for his teammates. He finished an impressive 25th overall, catching up to Cambridge's #4 runner in the closing stretch.

Kieran Morrissey-Dhenke was the squad's #6 runner on the day. It's hard to ask so much from a young freshman, but he still put together an amazing performance. Kieran placed 50th overall, ending his campaign as one of the top freshmen in the state. Toby Paradise then rounded out the squad with an impressive 92nd-place finish.

Congratulations to the Warriors on claiming their 5th consecutive All-State title. #DriveFor5

Spotlight: Cambridge Rindge & Latin

Cambridge Rindge & Latin looked pretty similar to how they did last week. It seems like this team just couldn't quite figure out how to put everything together in the postseason, which unfortunately for them is something Brookline has continued to excel at.

They still had some great individual performances. Leon Ibanez-Fraile bounced back this week with a big 11th-place finish to lead the squad. Thomas Dewitt followed in 17th, while Liam Eldridge also rebounded to place 21st overall.

However, in the final half-mile, Cambridge's score increased by 15 points, which ultimately contributed to its runner-up finish.

Spotlight: BC High

BC High also performed pretty similarly to last week, but it's amazing to see this team finish 4th overall after the start it had back in September.

There were some ups and downs among the athletes, but most notably, Colin Kurtz had a great day to place 8th overall, while Joseph Hayes followed in 23rd.

Considering everything this team went through earlier in the season, it's pretty satisfying to see it end its journey as one of the state's top teams. Finishing 4th overall at the Meet of Champions is quite the accomplishment.

Spotlight: North Andover & Natick

North Andover and Natick finished 5th and 6th overall, with North Andover taking the higher spot on the sixth-runner tiebreaker.

Nonetheless, both teams put together great performances and really rebounded from last weekend. Both squads beat all three D1B teams that had finished ahead of them the week before, making some pretty big statements.

North Andover had an impressive 32-second spread from its first through fifth runners and was led by Tucker LaRosa and Liam Rand. Cam Riccio rounded out the squad as its #6 runner, securing the tiebreaker win over Natick.

This team in particular had an amazing season. We didn't even have North Andover on our radar at the beginning of the year, but it ended its season with a huge 5th-place finish at the Meet of Champions, beating every other team from D1B.

Natick also put together a great performance. After having them ranked around #15 for most of the season, this race really showed us what they're capable of when everything comes together.

Callum Doyle continued his momentum from last weekend, placing 6th overall in an incredibly stacked field. Congratulations to him on really turning his season around.

It's also worth mentioning that several runners ended their seasons a bit early, which may explain some of the team scores that surprised people.

Jack Weintraub's absence affected Xaverian's score, Finn Johnson-Houlihan's absence affected Reading's, Devon McStowe's absence affected Oliver Ames', Max Gorski's absence affected Billerica's, and Brandon Ma's absence affected Lincoln-Sudbury's.

Individual Performances

On the individual side, there were also a plethora of outstanding performances.

The Billerica duo continued its momentum, going 1-2 to close out the season at the Meet of Champions. Mason Niles had a gutsy finish to become your All-State champion, while Sahil Gandhi outkicked Hartmann in the final few meters to take home runner-up honors. Truly a huge day for these two runners.

Landon Zoss also put together a great performance, finishing 4th overall, right behind Hartmann. We predicted that this race would come down to these four runners, and they proved exactly why.

Anthony Malakidis fell a bit further behind the Billerica duo this week but still put together an amazing performance. Malakidis finished 7th overall in a time of 15:56.

Patrick Geagan had a big day, bouncing back from last weekend. Geagan finished 10th overall, outkicking Ibanez-Fraile in the closing stretch.

However, our star performer of this race goes to Alex Pinnix from Sharon.

Pinnix truly embodied what it means to leave everything out on the course. He wasn't even on our radar heading into the meet, so seeing him actually lead the race through the first lap was a huge surprise. Even more impressively, Pinnix managed to hold on for a 13th-place finish.

Congratulations to this athlete on ending his season with such an incredible race.

Two more runners surprised us by earning the final spots on the individual podium. Will Olmstead led the Belmont trio this week with a 14th-place finish, while Stephen Green bounced back from last weekend to take the final podium spot in 15th.

What a way to close out the Division 1 season.

Division 2

Spotlight: Weston

What an unbelievable race from Weston.

Coming into the Meet of Champions as our #12-ranked team in the state, Weston knew it would have to put together something special to take down the heavy favorite, Longmeadow. And somehow, they did exactly that.

With just half a mile remaining, Weston was actually down by 25 points. At that point, it looked like Longmeadow was on its way to finally capturing the state title it had been chasing for so long. But Weston wasn't ready to give up.

In an incredible final stretch, Weston managed to erase that deficit and take home the Division 2 All-State title by just 2 points, 102-104. One of the most impressive comebacks we've seen all season.

Leading the squad was Matthew Fitzgibbon, who put together an amazing performance to place 7th overall in 16:18. After dealing with injuries throughout the beginning of the season, it's pretty incredible to see Fitzgibbon return to form at just the right time. He ended his season as Weston's #1 runner on the biggest stage of the year.

Hunter Waechter also had a great day, placing 10th overall in 16:22. Having two runners finish inside the top 10 was huge for Weston, especially against a Longmeadow squad with two of the best runners in the state.

But the performances that really stood out to us came from Jonah Watkins and Kevin Han.

Watkins, just a freshman, put together an incredible race to place 24th overall in 16:41. For a runner so young to step up like that at the Meet of Champions is pretty remarkable, and it gives Weston a lot to be excited about for the future.

However, Kevin Han might have been the most important runner in this entire race.

Han finished 35th overall in 17:02, scoring 29 points for Weston. With the team title coming down to just two points, every position mattered. While Fitzgibbon and Waechter were battling up front, Han's performance helped give Weston the points it needed to pull off the upset.

It's easy to focus on the runners at the front when a team wins a championship, but this race was a perfect example of why every single runner matters. Without Han stepping up, there's a very good chance Weston doesn't walk away with that trophy.

Aarav Mehta then rounded out the scoring five with a 50th-place finish in 17:17, securing the final points Weston needed to complete the comeback.

Congratulations to Weston on an incredible race and one of the biggest upsets of the season.

Spotlight: Longmeadow

This one is pretty heartbreaking.

Longmeadow came into this meet as the clear #1 seed in Division 2. After dominating the conference and divisional championships, everything seemed to be lining up for this team to finally capture an All-State title.

And for most of the race, it looked like that was exactly what would happen.

Up front, CJ Deliso and Jacob Abebe were putting together two of the best performances of the entire meet. The Longmeadow duo went 1-2, with Deliso taking home the individual state title in 15:55 and Abebe following just four seconds behind in 15:59.

It's hard to ask for much more than that. Having two runners take the first two scoring spots should put just about any team in an incredible position to win.

Aidan Kretschmar followed as Longmeadow's #3 runner, placing 32nd overall in 16:54. Finn O'Connor came through in 37th at 17:07, while Henry Cournoyer rounded out the scoring five in 56th with a time of 17:25.

The team finished with 104 points, just two behind Weston.

What makes this result so difficult is what happened to Longmeadow last year.

At the 2025 Meet of Champions, Longmeadow lost the Division 2 title to Boston Latin Academy by just one point. After coming so close, this season felt like their opportunity to come back and finally finish the job.

Instead, they found themselves on the wrong side of another incredibly close race.

Losing a state championship by one point is tough enough. Coming back the following year as the favorite, only to lose by two points, is something that's hard to even imagine.

But there's one huge positive for this team.

Longmeadow is returning its entire scoring squad next year.

Deliso, Abebe, Kretschmar, O'Connor, and Cournoyer will all have another opportunity to chase that title. After everything this group has experienced over the past two seasons, it's hard to imagine a team coming into next year with more motivation.

This result definitely hurts, but Longmeadow's story is far from over.

Spotlight: Boston Latin Academy

Boston Latin Academy might have had one of the most satisfying performances of the day.

Coming into this meet, BLA wasn't really being talked about as one of the main contenders for a trophy. With Longmeadow, Weston, and Groton-Dunstable getting most of the attention, Boston Latin Academy had something to prove.

And they delivered.

The team's top four runners, all seniors, put together an incredible final race to help BLA take 3rd overall with 166 points.

Adam Kramer and Bradon Spiess led the way with two huge performances. Kramer placed 5th overall in 16:13, while Spiess followed right behind in 6th with a time of 16:18.

Nathaniel Iliff also had a great day, finishing 17th overall in 16:31. Khalid Jama then came through in 30th at 16:49.

That's four runners inside the top 30 at the Meet of Champions.

The problem for BLA has always been finding that fifth runner to back up its incredible front four. There's only so much you can do when you have four runners competing at such a high level but a much larger gap to your fifth.

Still, these four seniors did everything they could.

Even with that gap, Boston Latin Academy managed to upset Groton-Dunstable and take home the final team trophy. That's a pretty incredible accomplishment considering how competitive this Division 2 field was.

After winning the state title last year, it was great to see these seniors close out their high school cross-country careers with another podium finish.

Congratulations to this group on an amazing final season.

Spotlight: Groton-Dunstable

Although Groton-Dunstable came into the meet with hopes of taking home a trophy, things took an unfortunate turn when their top runner, Andrew Kosiba, dropped out of the race.

For a team that relies so heavily on its front runners, losing its #1 in the middle of the biggest race of the season is a huge setback.

But the rest of the squad didn't give up.

Micah Pierantozzi stepped up in a big way, leading Groton-Dunstable with a 15th-place finish in 16:26. He was followed by Greyson Duane, who placed 19th overall in 16:35.

Pierantozzi's performance was especially impressive. With Kosiba out of the race, someone needed to step up and take control for the team. Pierantozzi answered that call, putting together a performance that helped keep Groton-Dunstable in contention.

Ashton Duane followed in 40th place with a time of 17:08, while Ashvik Yadav and Cameron Duane rounded out the scoring five.

Despite losing their top runner, Groton-Dunstable still managed to finish 4th overall with 174 points, just eight behind Boston Latin Academy.

It's pretty amazing that this team was still able to finish so high without one of its most important athletes. Although they narrowly missed the podium, Groton-Dunstable proved just how much depth this squad has.

Individual Performances

On the individual side, Division 2 gave us plenty of great performances.

Of course, the biggest story was the Longmeadow duo of CJ Deliso and Jacob Abebe.

After going back and forth throughout the season, these two finally got to finish their cross-country campaigns together at the very front of the state championship. Deliso took the individual crown in 15:55, while Abebe followed in 15:59 to complete the 1-2 finish.

Although the team result wasn't what they wanted, these two deserve a ton of credit for the seasons they put together.

Another runner who really impressed us was Alexander Hamlin from Masconomet.

Hamlin put together an incredible race to finish 3rd overall in 16:05, earning himself a spot on the podium against some of the best runners in Massachusetts.

After Longmeadow took the first two spots, Hamlin was the next runner across the line. That's a huge accomplishment, especially in a field this competitive.

The East Longmeadow duo also deserves some recognition.

Nick Rivera continued his incredible postseason momentum, finishing 4th overall in 16:06. After capturing the Division 2B individual title last weekend, Rivera proved once again that he belongs among the state's best.

But the performance that really stood out to us was Zachary Mazzaferro.

Mazzaferro didn't have his best race at the divisional championship last weekend, but he completely turned things around at the Meet of Champions. He finished 12th overall in 16:23, putting together a huge bounce-back performance to close out his season.

It's always great to see an athlete rebound after a disappointing race, especially when it happens on the biggest stage of the year.

Benjamin Barron from Whitinsville Christian also had a great day, finishing 8th overall in 16:21, while Amherst's Calvin Miller followed in 9th with a time of 16:21.

Division 2 truly had everything this year. An incredible comeback, a heartbreaking runner-up finish, some major individual performances, and a championship decided by just two points.

What a way to end the season.

Division 3

Spotlight: Hopedale

What an unbelievable way for Hopedale to finish its season.

Coming into the Meet of Champions as the clear favorite in Division 3, we knew this team had the potential to put together something special. But what they accomplished on Saturday was beyond impressive.

Hopedale absolutely dominated the field, scoring just 44 points to take home the Division 3 All-State title. Not only was that the lowest winning score across all three divisions, but all five of Hopedale's scoring runners finished inside the top 20 overall.

Just an incredible performance from top to bottom.

Leading the way was Quinn Cook, who put the finishing touches on one of the most impressive seasons we've seen from a Massachusetts sophomore. Cook took home the individual All-State title in 15:49, adding another major victory to his already incredible season.

After taking wins at the Frank Kelley Invitational, Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational, Frank Mooney Invitational, and CMAC Championship, Cook had already established himself as one of the best runners in the state. But winning the Meet of Champions was the perfect way to close out his season.

And he did it against some serious competition.

Evan Hedlund, Zephyr Schermerhorn, and Trevor Sullivan were all expected to challenge for the individual crown, but Cook proved why he was our favorite going into the meet.

Though Hopedale's dominance went far beyond its #1 runner.

Ben Stone had another incredible performance, placing 5th overall in 16:13. Stone has been one of the most consistent runners on this squad, and after finishing 4th at last year's Meet of Champions, he once again proved that he belongs among the best runners in Division 3.

Connor Fitzgibbon also put together a great race, finishing 13th overall in 16:29. Having three runners finish inside the top 15 was already a huge advantage for Hopedale, but what really made this team special was the depth behind them.

And that's where Jack Murphy comes in.

Murphy might have had the race of his life on Saturday.

Usually sitting a bit farther back in Hopedale's lineup, Murphy stepped up when his team needed him most. He finished an incredible 17th overall in 16:33, setting a personal best and becoming the team's #4 runner on the day.

We talk so much about the top runners on championship teams, but performances like Murphy's are what really separate the great teams from everyone else. For someone who usually finishes farther back in the lineup to step up like that at the biggest meet of the year is pretty remarkable.

Cedric Arthur followed just behind Murphy, placing 18th overall in 16:34, also running a personal best. With Arthur rounding out the scoring five, Hopedale had every single one of its scorers across the line before most teams even had their third runner finish.

That's just ridiculous depth.

What's even more impressive is that Hopedale accomplished all of this while missing one of its top runners for the entire season due to injury.

For a team to lose such an important piece of its lineup and still put together a performance like this says a lot about the work these athletes have put in.

And here's something that really puts this performance into perspective.

Hopedale finished with a combined team time of 1:21:40, averaging exactly 16:20 per runner. That's actually faster than Cambridge Rindge & Latin's team time in Division 1.

Think about that for a second.

At the beginning of the season, we didn't even have Hopedale ranked among the state's top teams. Now, after everything this squad has accomplished, they finish the season as our #2 team in Massachusetts.

That's an incredible jump.

It's easy to look at the final results and see a team that dominated its division, but there's so much more behind this performance. From Cook leading the way, to Stone and Fitzgibbon delivering up front, to Murphy and Arthur stepping up in a huge way, every single member of this team played a part.

Hopedale didn't just win a state championship. They made one of the biggest statements of the entire season.

Congratulations to the Hopedale boys on an unbelievable year.

Spotlight: Parker Charter

Although Parker Charter couldn't quite keep up with Hopedale on Saturday, this was still a really impressive performance from the three-time defending state champions.

Coming into the season, Parker had been dealing with its fair share of injuries and setbacks. After winning three consecutive All-State titles, this year was always going to be a challenge.

But this team still found a way to finish as the Division 3 runner-up with 98 points.

And a lot of that came down to two incredible performances up front.

Zephyr Schermerhorn had one of the best races of his entire season, finishing 3rd overall in a personal-best 16:04.

After taking home the Division 3C individual title at Northfield Mountain last weekend, Schermerhorn continued that momentum into the Meet of Champions. He came into this race looking like one of the biggest threats to the favorites, and he absolutely delivered.

His performance was especially important for Parker Charter. With Hopedale putting so many runners near the front, Parker needed its top athletes to score as few points as possible to stay in contention.

Schermerhorn did exactly that.

Jett Johnson also put together an amazing race, finishing 9th overall in 16:21. Johnson has been one of the most reliable runners on this team throughout the season, and once again he delivered when it mattered most.

Having two runners inside the top 10 was a huge accomplishment, especially against a Hopedale squad that was having the race of its life.

Nathan Davis followed with a personal-best 16:52, finishing 26th overall. Asher Schermerhorn then came through in 37th at 17:11, while Danny Garrison rounded out the scoring five in 42nd with a time of 17:23.

Although Parker wasn't able to capture its fourth consecutive title, there's really nothing to be disappointed about with this performance.

Finishing runner-up at the Meet of Champions during an injury-heavy season is still a huge accomplishment. This team has built an incredible championship culture over the past several years, and Saturday showed that Parker Charter is still one of the strongest programs in Division 3.

Congratulations to Parker Charter on another great season.

Spotlight: Mount Greylock

Mount Greylock also deserves a ton of credit for its performance on Saturday.

Coming into the Meet of Champions, most of the attention was on Hopedale and Parker Charter, but Mount Greylock put together a great race to take home the final team trophy.

The squad finished 3rd overall with 158 points, comfortably ahead of Greater Lowell Tech in 4th.

Patrick Holland led the way with a 24th-place finish in 16:51, followed by Luke Irwin, who placed 27th in 16:57.

Finnegan Voisin also had a great day, finishing 31st overall in 17:04. Having three runners finish within about 13 seconds of each other was a huge advantage for Mount Greylock.

Andrew Holland followed in 54th, while Benjamin Mandel rounded out the scoring five in 66th.

Mount Greylock might not have had the same star power up front as Hopedale or Parker Charter, but its depth and consistency were enough to secure a spot on the podium.

A great way for this team to close out its season.

Individual Performances

On the individual side, Division 3 gave us some incredible races.

Of course, the biggest performance came from Quinn Cook, who took home the individual All-State title in 15:49.

Cook has been one of the most dominant runners in Massachusetts throughout the fall, and Saturday was the perfect way to end his season. With another championship added to his resume, it's pretty amazing to think that he's still only a sophomore.

But the runner who really impressed us was Evan Hedlund from Frontier Regional.

After a somewhat inconsistent regular season, Hedlund really found his form when the postseason arrived. He took home the Division 3B title last weekend and followed it up with an incredible runner-up finish at the Meet of Champions.

Hedlund ran 15:58, becoming one of just two runners in Division 3 to break 16 minutes on the day.

We knew Hedlund was capable of a performance like this, especially considering he was the #1 returner from last year's Meet of Champions. Still, seeing him put everything together in the final two races of the season was pretty special.

Zephyr Schermerhorn also deserves another mention for his 3rd-place finish in 16:04. After his breakout performance at the divisional championship, he managed to take things even further on Saturday, running a personal best against the strongest field in the division.

Another athlete who really surprised us was Harrison Mello from Hudson.

Mello put together a great race to finish 4th overall in 16:09, placing ahead of several runners who had been getting much more attention throughout the season.

Trevor Sullivan from Granby also closed out his season with a strong performance, placing 6th overall in 16:16. Sullivan has been one of the top sophomores in Massachusetts all season, and this was another great result against some of the state's best competition.

Cassidy McBee from Triton Regional had a big day as well, finishing 7th overall in 16:17. Ben Harwood from Lenox followed right behind in 8th, just two-tenths of a second later.

Konrad Schluter from Maynard also deserves some recognition. Schluter finished 11th overall in 16:24, putting together another impressive race to close out his season.

And we can't forget Ben McWaters from Bromfield, who finished 12th overall in 16:25.

Division 3 was truly Hopedale's day. From Quinn Cook taking home the individual crown to all five scorers finishing inside the top 20, this was one of the most complete team performances we've seen all year.

For a squad that wasn't even on our radar at the beginning of the season to finish as one of the best teams in Massachusetts is pretty remarkable.

What an incredible way to close out the 2026 cross-country season.
