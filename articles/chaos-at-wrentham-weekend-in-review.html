Chaos at Wrentham: Weekend in Review

Major upsets, breakout performances, and our best look yet at some of the top teams and individuals in Massachusetts.

By Ethan Barossi · October 4, 2026
North Andover boys after a big win in the Varsity B race.

With major upsets, big victories, and our best look at some of the top teams and individuals in the state, this weekend showed what Massachusetts cross country is really about. Here we’ll break down all the action you missed this weekend.

MSTCA Frank Kelley Invitational

Spotlight - Hopedale

In the morning, some of the top teams in the state ventured up to Wrentham for the open 5K. On the team side, the Hopedale boys had a big victory over Northampton, getting them in the last lap of the Wrentham course. Although both teams entered with a really tight pack, Hopedale’s strength was just how tight their pack truly was.

Hopedale’s 2-4 spread was only 8 whole seconds, getting all 5 runners in the top 15. Ben Stone (16:38), the #3 returner from the Division 3 Meet of Champs, led this pack to place 10th overall. He was then followed by 3 more teammates who all passed 2 Northampton runners in the last bit of the course. Connor Fitzgibbon (16:39) placed 12th, Cedric Author (16:40) was right behind in 13th, and Jack Murphy (16:46) rounded out the squad in 15th.

Though it’s not only Hopedale’s pack that guides them, it’s their top end too. In an absolute thriller of a race, Hopedale’s Quinn Cook (15:38) took the individual title in the first new MA #1 of the day. Cook appeared to be pacing most of the race, with Landon Zoss (15:44) giving chase right behind him. It was clear the race would be between these two runners from the first lap. During the final lap, Zoss made a surge up the hill and Cook didn’t have a response. Cook then found another gear in the last bits of the course to take away the title.

Hopedale is now a very serious threat. Their top-end talent and tight pack make them one of the best teams in Division 3. Will they be the ones to finally take down Parker Charter?

Spotlight - Northampton

Although only placing 2nd, Northampton also ran one hell of a race. Although they don’t have much top-end talent, they have a great pack with a 36-second spread from 1-5 and a 10-second spread from 1-3. Henry Daggot (16:29) and Sha O’Neil (16:35) led the squad, followed by Gus Frey (16:50), Owen Daggot (16:50), and Charlie Sidoti (17:06).

This team was not even on our radar for Division 2 teams but now has our attention. Based on all their performances from yesterday, they currently jump to the #2 seed. We’re excited to see what these boys do later in the season, within Western Mass and Division 2.

MSTCA Bay State Twilight Invitational

Disclaimer - During the Twilight meet, there were plenty of claims that Wrentham was actually long this year by around 30-50 meters. There’s plenty of Strava data to back up these claims.

On to the races, it was a big night.

A Race

Spotlight - Brookline

Brookline is back. But when were they ever gone? The Warriors ran their first all-out 5K on Saturday night and proved their strength. Even with a handful of their runners not running their best race, they were still the best team that raced on the Wrentham course this weekend.

Liam Hartmann (15:23) and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem (15:35) placed 5th and 6th overall to prove themselves against some of the best runners in the state. Though it is still important to note that if any of the top individuals can run faster times, it should be these two.

Then came the Brookline pack with some exceptional performances. Elias Thomasson seems to have broken away from the pack, as he had a big race to run 16:09 and place 15th overall. Two more Warriors, Toby Paradise (16:15) and Matthew Ritter (16:16), placed in the top 25 to close out the team. Nam Danh and Noah Karp are also usually key members of this pack, but it appears they just didn’t have their best day.

Three more Warriors rounded out Brookline’s team top 10 to add some key depth. Peter Clark (16:41), Aidan Wotzak (16:43), and Wei Chandler (16:51) all finished within the top 50.

Although this appeared like it could’ve been a close meet between Brookline and Concord-Carlisle, the Warriors destroyed the competition. 3 Brookline runners were in front of Concord’s #1 and all 5 were in front of their #2.

Spotlight - Concord-Carlisle

Still, this is a great effort for the Concord-Carlisle boys. Their ideals of depth and pack running make this team look strong heading into October.

Braeden Quayle led the squad, placing 16th with a time of 16:04. Toby Martel (16:10), a runner who’s better known for his mid-distance capabilities, followed closely behind to place 21st.

Though after that, their true pack came into play. Elijah Liang (16:24) placed 28th, Sawyer Bout (16:25) placed 29th, Will Okie (16:29) placed 32nd, Ryan Perry (16:31) placed 33rd, Nick Baughman (16:32) placed 34th, and Jack Thomas (16:32) placed 35th. That’s 8 seconds separating 6 runners who are putting down some amazing times.

Spotlight - Lincoln-Sudbury and Newton South

Overlooked and underestimated: The Dual County League is getting heated. Lincoln-Sudbury and Newton South placed 3rd and 4th overall with some huge times from their top runners.

Lincoln-Sudbury has some crazy top-end talent. Josh Gamel (15:50), Lukas Masiakos (15:55), and Brandon Ma (15:56) all crossed the line together to place 9-10-11 overall. We knew this trio was going to be fast, but we’re still in shock at how fast they are. They are definitely moving up the rankings after this performance.

Tasker Mendelson (16:49) and Ben Scanga (17:16) rounded out this squad. Although they’re a bit farther back, their top end helps negate this, as proven by their win over conference rival Newton South.

Though be on the lookout for Lincoln-Sudbury’s #6 runner, Andrew Sholk (17:25). He’s only a freshman and has a familiar last name from Massachusetts distance running history.

Only 7 points behind them was Newton South. As opposed to Lincoln-Sudbury, where their strength is top-end and back-end, South’s strength is in their pack.

We predicted that Newton South would have a good trio of runners, but there are now 4 up front, with a freshman leading their squad. Jordi Martorell (16:09) led the squad, followed by his older brother, Fran Martorell (16:14), then Cooper McFarland (16:22) and Carl Munshi (16:22), finishing with a 13-second spread from 1-4.

Alan Herring rounded out the squad in 16:59. Safe to say Newton South and Lincoln-Sudbury are moving way up in the rankings.

Spotlight - Individuals

We’ve been saying it from the beginning, but the Billerica duo are the top individuals to beat throughout the entire state. Sahil Gandhi (15:14) won the meet overall, followed by Mason Niles (15:16). They are currently MA #1 and #2. Their ability to train with each other gives them the edge that most other teams don’t have. Both runners sat patiently in the pack the whole race, not taking over until the final stretch.

Anthony Malakidis placed 3rd overall in 15:20. We expected him to be in this position, as he is one of the state’s top returners.

Granby’s Trevor Sullivan placed 4th and proved that his win over Evan Hudland earlier in the season wasn’t a fluke. He is now the clear favorite for Division 3 and a sophomore who’s competing with the best individuals in the state.

One of the East Longmeadow runners had a breakout race, with his teammate a bit farther back. Nick Rivera placed 7th in 15:41, while Zachary Mazzaferro placed 14th in 16:02. Keep your eyes on this duo for the future inside Division 2.

B Race

Spotlight - North Andover

The biggest team of the night was undoubtedly North Andover. Whatever we thought of this team before Saturday is not even close to where they are now. North Andover is now a top 3 team in the state after a huge upset over St. John’s Prep.

Their main pack went from the 16:30 range to having 3 runners under the 16:00 barrier. Will Thomas (15:52) led the squad, followed by Tucker LaRose (15:57) and Liam Rand (15:59). These 3 boys had only a 7-second spread, with them going 10-12 in a very stacked race. Owen Desmand (16:11), Tristan Rand (16:20), and Cameron Riccio (16:28) rounded out this squad. L. Rand, Desmand, and Riccio are currently the best #3, #4, and #6 in the state.

You thought that was deep? North Andover has more to give. Nate Zwinggi (16:35), Luka Radivojevic (16:36), and Carter Eastwick-Haskall (16:37) also all placed within the top 40. Two of these runners won’t be competing in the postseason, which is absolutely absurd.

The boys were absolutely pumped over their victory, especially because they were right about us sleeping on them. We mentioned that D1B was going to be tight, but this race might’ve sealed the deal. The boys jump from the middle of the pack to the #1 seed for the race. We're excited to see where this team goes and what they can accomplish.

Spotlight - St. John’s Prep

Though this was not at all a bad effort for St. John’s Prep, hardly from it actually. It appears that one of their top runners, Henry McDonnell (16:38), was in the JV race. Do we know why? No, though we still think North Andover would’ve taken it, even with him in the race.

Prep also had a trio of runners under 16:00: Leo Sentenrich (15:42), Mateo DeOrio (15:50), and Liam Callahan (15:59). From there, the team’s depth might’ve been a bit inconsistent with what we’ve seen from them earlier in the season. Sam Valentine (16:34) and Colton Hayhurst (16:57) rounded out this squad.

If this team can get their depth back in check, they will still be on the same level as teams like Brookline and North Andover.

Spotlight - Parker Charter

The boys from Parker Charter opened their season last night in the B race to place third. If you thought Hopedale was deep for a Division 3 team, you haven’t seen this group. Jett Johnson led the squad to run 15:50, placing 8th overall. It’ll be interesting to see how he matches up with Hopedale’s Cook this year, as both were some of the state’s top freshmen in 2025.

3 more Parker runners then crossed the line together, with no other runners in between them. Danny Garrison (16:16) led the trio, with Cypress Lance (16:17) and Zephyr Schermerhorn (16:19) following closely behind. Nathan Davis (16:54) rounded out this squad.

Watch out, Division 3. Parker Charter is back and ready. We’re excited to see if the Hopedale pack can get up and challenge the Parker Charter pack, but for now they do have the edge.

Spotlight - Boston Latin Academy

BLA’s quartet looks scary. These boys opened their season with dominance and the desire to take back their title. Brandon Spiess (15:34) placed 3rd overall against some heavy individual talent. Adam Kramer then had a huge breakout race to place 7th, running 15:46. Khalid Jama ran 16:39, and Nate Iliff then came in at 17:01. Iliff especially did not look as though he had his best day.

Though their main problem is still their biggest setback. Xavier Lee (18:10) came in at 100th place overall. If their depth problem gets solved, this team has the complete ability to take back their title, but it’s hard to be one of the top teams in the state when your #5 is in the 18:00 range.

Spotlight - Weston

Weston placed 8th. What’s so special about them? They didn’t race their top 2 runners, who are both below the 16:00 mark.

If Weston has both those two runners healthy, that duo is now a trio. Hunter Waechter (15:37) placed 4th overall, running a huge PR. When we were watching the race unfold, we actually thought he was his teammate Mathew Fitzgibbon. So when we saw this result, we were especially surprised.

Weston also has a crazy freshman, Jonah Watkins (16:31), who placed 31st overall on the Wrentham course. Kevin Han placed third on the squad and ran 16:50.

If this entire lineup is together, they also have the potential to take the Division 2 title.

Spotlight - Individuals

Andrew Kosiba won this race in a blistering time of 15:30. Kosiba is the #1 returner for Division 2 and proved it Saturday night.

His teammate, Greyson Duane (16:19), ran in the morning at the Frank Kelley Invite. We were expecting these 2 runners to be a bit closer, but they still combine for a heavy 1-2 punch.

Weymouth’s Patrick Geagan placed 2nd overall in 15:34, outleaning Spiess from BLA at the line. This doesn’t look like Geagan’s best day, and we believe he has the potential to run a lot faster.
