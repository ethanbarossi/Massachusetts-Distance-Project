2026 Division 2: What to Expect

Unlike Division 1, the team that will win the overall Division 2 title is a bit more up in the air. With three main contenders all competing in different divisions, they won't face off just yet. However, this weekend will give us our best look at who has the potential to win it all.

Division 2A

Team Seedings

  1. Longmeadow
  2. Boston Latin Academy
  3. Walpole
  4. Masconomet Regional

Chase Pack: Melrose, Marblehead, Canton, Essex North Shore

Longmeadow seems to be the clear favorite here if they run all their guys. They currently have all five runners under 17:00, with their top two runners, CJ Deliso and Jacob Abebe, under the 15:30 mark. They have the top-end talent to compete with Boston Latin Academy, along with the back-end depth that Boston lacks.

Boston Latin Academy slides into second here, but the main battle for runner-up will be between them, Walpole, and Masconomet. We're pretty confident that Boston can get all four of its top runners into the top 10, but its No. 5 runner is just too far back. Their fifth runner comes in around the 50th seed, with Walpole placing all seven runners ahead of him. That's also without key teams like Masconomet and Marblehead having put down seed times yet.

Walpole does have its own problems, though. They've had a somewhat inconsistent season, similar to Reading, but they've still put down some impressive times and currently hold four of the top 15 seeds.

Division 2A is easily one of the most team-heavy divisions this year, with Boston Latin Academy, Longmeadow, and Walpole holding 13 of the top 15 seeds. We're extremely excited to see how these three teams battle it out.

Still, keep an eye on Masconomet this year. They currently don't have any seed times, but they look pretty strong judging by the team's 3K performances earlier in the season at the Frank Kelly Invitational.

Key Individuals

  • Bradon Spiess — Boston Latin Academy
  • Adam Kramer — Boston Latin Academy
  • Nate Iliff — Boston Latin Academy
  • CJ Deliso — Longmeadow
  • Jacob Abebe — Longmeadow
  • Finn O'Connor — Longmeadow
  • Nate O'Neil — Walpole
  • Alexander Hamlin — Masconomet

We could see Spiess, Kramer, Deliso, Abebe, or Hamlin all walking away with the individual title. However, the main battle will most likely be between Bradon Spiess and CJ Deliso, as both are incredible runners with very similar skill sets.

Neither of their teammates is that far behind, though. Jacob Abebe recently beat Deliso in a dual meet, while Adam Kramer outleaned Spiess at the HOKA Postal Nationals two-mile a couple of weeks ago.

Division 2B

Team Seedings

  1. Groton-Dunstable
  2. Amherst-Pelham
  3. Northampton
  4. Pembroke

Chase Pack: Ashland, Wayland, Burlington, Wakefield Memorial, Ludlow

On paper, it looks like a close battle between Groton-Dunstable and Amherst-Pelham, but we don't really think that's the case. The Western Mass course appears to have been a bit short, making Amherst look faster on paper. Meanwhile, Greyson Duane from Groton-Dunstable hasn't put down a time that reflects his full potential this season, making Groton look slower.

That's not to say Amherst is out of it, though. Far from it, actually. Last year, Groton-Dunstable pulled off an upset over Amherst at this meet at Northfield Mountain, so you can expect Amherst to be looking for some revenge. Although those times came on a fast course, Calvin Miller, Peter Nedeau, and Otis Fairey all enter with PRs under 16:00.

Groton-Dunstable also has a strong trio up front in Andrew Kosiba, Greyson Duane, and Micah Pierantozzi. Expect both teams to battle it out at the front.

Northampton slides into third here, though they're definitely not a team to be underestimated. They enter with four runners under 16:30, three of whom are in podium contention. Henry Daggett and Shea O&#x27;Neil lead the squad up front.

Key Individuals

  • Andrew Kosiba — Groton-Dunstable
  • Greyson Duane — Groton-Dunstable
  • Calvin Miller — Amherst-Pelham
  • Peter Nedeau — Amherst-Pelham
  • Nate Palmieri — Pembroke
  • Zachary Mazzaferro — East Longmeadow
  • Nick Rivera — East Longmeadow
  • Luca Domestico — Ashland
  • Nordeen Fadil — Newburyport

Division 2B is easily the most individually competitive part of Division 2. We'd consider Andrew Kosiba the clear favorite after his dominant performance at the Bay State Invitational, though he did struggle a bit at the Twilight Invitational.

If Kosiba isn't on his game, Calvin Miller or the East Longmeadow duo should be next in line. Recently, at the Western Mass Championship, Nick Rivera placed third overall, followed by Miller in fourth and Zachary Mazzaferro in fifth. Expect a tight battle up front.

Don't sleep on Nate Palmieri from Pembroke either. He hasn't put down a standout time yet, but we know he's a very strong runner.

Division 2C

Team Seedings

  1. Weston
  2. Whitinsville Christian
  3. Dover-Sherborn

Division 2C is one of, if not the most, straightforward divisions of all nine. Weston should comfortably take home the title, with no team looking ready to challenge them. They currently hold four of the top 15 seeds, and Aarav Mehta, who is still working his way back into shape, isn't even one of them. We really don't see a scenario where Weston doesn't conquer this division.

Whitinsville Christian comes in next. Again, this seeding should be pretty straightforward. They enter well behind Weston but comfortably ahead of Dover-Sherborn. We expect them to take home the runner-up trophy without much trouble. Benjamin Barron leads the squad as the No. 2 seed in the race.

Key Individuals

  • Hunter Waechter — Weston
  • Matthew Fitzgibbon — Weston
  • Aarav Mehta — Weston
  • Jonah Watkins — Weston
  • Benjamin Barron — Whitinsville Christian
  • Henry Humphreys — Dedham
  • Cooper Hunt — Wilmington
  • Jackson Doyle — Dover-Sherborn

Hunter Waechter seems to be the clear favorite after being Weston's No. 1 runner all season. He's had some standout performances at the Bay State Invitational and Twilight Invitational, where he took home first place.

However, Benjamin Barron is one hell of a runner, too. He finished runner-up to Waechter at the Twilight Invitational and boasts a PR of 15:34. If Waechter is off his game, we can absolutely expect Barron to be in contention for the individual title.

Waechter's teammate, Matthew Fitzgibbon, is also in contention for the individual title. After what looks to have been an injury-heavy season, he finished just eight seconds behind Waechter at their conference meet, clocking a time of 16:03 in the tough conditions at Franklin Park.

Fitzgibbon is rounding back into form at the right time and should be a key player for the squad on Saturday.
