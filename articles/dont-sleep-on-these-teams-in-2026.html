Don’t Sleep on These Teams in 2026

The MDP rankings only go 15 teams deep, but there are plenty of other squads across Massachusetts capable of making noise this fall.

By Ethan BarossiSeptember 4, 2026

Although our team rankings will only include the top 15 during the season, there are still plenty of other teams in the state that can make some noise. Many of these teams are either returning deep squads or have characteristics that aren't necessarily shown on paper. This article will cover some more of the top teams in the state and what you can expect from them this season.

Disclaimer: This is not a ranking, but simply a list of teams.

Amherst-Pelham and Arlington both belong in this group of squads. However, we already talked about them in one of our preseason rankings articles. If you wish to know more about those teams, you can find them here.

Billerica

In our opinion, heading into the season, Billerica probably has the best 1-2 punch in the state. Mason Niles (9:18) has one of the better chances at becoming an All-State champion. Niles was injured toward the end of last cross country season and didn’t compete in the postseason. However, he likely would’ve finished inside the top ten if he competed at the Meet of Champions.

Sahil Gandhi (9:13) is right with Niles and actually holds a better two-mile PR, beating him at the Weston Twilight Invitational this spring.

Their No. 3 runner is actually an incoming freshman. How do we know this? Because he ran a 4:38 mile in eighth grade. Dylan Kane enters the season and should easily be able to take down his 17:34 PR. Christopher Panzeri (17:35) and Hayden Headley (17:49) round out this squad as strong No. 4 and No. 5 runners.

Needham

After a few down years, the Needham program seems to be back with a strong pack of runners. It looks like the squad will be led by Austin Conti (16:35) and Connor Bobbin (4:39, 17:10).

After that, there are several athletes who could take the next few spots. Although injured last year, Cash Lindsley (4:47, 10:16) and Kaden Hu (2:00, 1:28) both return to the lineup this year.

Gabriele Mineo (17:24), Zayan Hussain (17:33), Duncan Alexander (17:41), and Harrison O’Garr (10:47) could also all be heavy hitters for the squad.

Be on the lookout for incoming sophomores Caleb Gilberth and Adam Pfaff. Both had great freshman campaigns, and we’re excited to see what they can do this year.

Newton South

We didn’t go over the Dual County League on this account, but even outside of the top three, there are still some top-tier teams.

Newton South returns three seniors below the 17:00 barrier. Patrick Feng (16:44), Harry Danila-Schmidt (16:46), and Carl Munshi (16:54) all return to lead this squad.

Junior Fran Tibau Martorall (17:06) is right behind them and might even be able to stick with the trio in some races. Kavin Sundaram (17:44) anchors this squad. Although his time isn’t top-notch, we believe the strength of the top four could help make up for it.

Belmont and Winchester

Next, the Belmont and Winchester rivalry should be an exciting part of Massachusetts running this year. Both have very similar teams and should challenge each other whenever they compete in the same meet.

Winchester probably has the edge over Belmont, but expect both teams’ top three runners to give each other a battle.

Winchester returns Noah Damaske (4:41), Tyler Bernstein (9:50), and Noah Mannelli (9:46). Belmont is returning Noah Geiger (9:26), Ishaan Kanagaraj (9:56), and William Olmsted (10:03).

However, Winchester’s edge isn’t necessarily its top three — it’s the team’s depth. Nikola Dimitrov (16:34) beats out Belmont’s No. 4 on paper, while Nate Bickford (17:01) beats out Belmont’s No. 5.

Boston Latin Academy

Your defending Division 2 state champions aren't done yet.

Although it appears this team is returning five of its top seven, one athlete, Terrance McGhee Jr., is actually transferring to BC High this year. Nonetheless, they are still a formidable squad and a team that knows how to perform in the postseason.

Brandon Spiess (15:47) returns for his senior year ready to compete for individual and team hardware. Spiess is the No. 3 returner from last year's Division 2 Meet of Champions.

Adam Kramer (16:22) is back as a really solid No. 2 runner for BLA. Kramer also flourished on the track, running 4:31 for the mile.

From there, they return two more runners who have run below the 10:00 barrier in the two-mile: Khalid Jama (9:46) and Nathaniel Iliff (9:48).

The team’s main problem is finding a solid No. 5. On paper, Mason Mastrodicasa returns to round out the squad with a PR of 18:19.

Can BLA go back-to-back?

Walpole

Walpole may return some of the most misleading statistics of any team in this article.

They already return three seniors below the 17:00 barrier: Asher Kingman (16:47), Owen Ahlfont (16:48), and Joseph Curran (16:59).

After that, their next runner, Nate O’Neil, has very misleading times. He returns with a season best of 17:23 and a PR of 17:16 from his sophomore year — a season in which he was well ahead of Kingman, Ahlfont, and Curran. He also enters with a 4:34 mile and 2:03 800m.

There’s a strong possibility we actually see O’Neil leading the Walpole boys this year.

It also looks like Josiah Escobar (18:33) comes in as their No. 5, but that could be misleading as well. Ethan Hamilton (10:46, 5:05) and Camden Triessel (5:06) have both never run a 5K before and are coming off very strong spring track seasons.

North Andover

The boys from North Andover are one of the faster squads on this list. They not only return five different runners below 17:05, but also a squad that doesn’t necessarily have a clear No. 1 runner.

Tucker Larosa (16:15, 9:50) returns with the fastest 5K on the team but only the third-best two-mile.

William Thomas (16:39, 9:49) and Liam Rand (16:51, 9:46) showed major improvement over this past track season and are ready to challenge Larosa for the top spot.

Thomas and Rand weren't the only ones to show massive improvement on the track. Their No. 4 and No. 5 runners, Tristan Rand (17:02, 10:07) and Owen Desmond (17:02, 10:02), also have phenomenal two-mile PRs and look capable of taking a good amount of time off their 5K marks.

The MVC should be scared of this team and what they can accomplish.

Xaverian Brothers

The Xaverian squad also brings back plenty of talent. Although overshadowed by the tremendous strength of both the Catholic Conference and Division 1B, they still return a very deep squad.

Jack Weintraub (16:32), although more known for the 800m, leads the Xaverian squad in the 5K as well.

Behind him are three more solid runners in Marco Petrino (16:43), Alex Armstrong (16:46), and Desmond Sullivan (17:05).

Although it looks like their No. 5 is a bit farther back, Scott Feeney comes in with a 17:53 5K and had an explosive track season in which he ran a 4:40 mile and 1:57 800m.

If Feeney can climb his way up the Xaverian squad, this team could look stronger than ever.

Lincoln-Sudbury

This is the fourth out of six teams in the Foley Division of the Dual County League that we will have our eyes on.

Lincoln-Sudbury’s strength is its top end. They have a solid 1-2 punch in Brandon Ma (16:24) and Lukas Masiakos (16:25).

On paper, their No. 3 is a bit farther back. However, he was only a freshman last year and put up some crazy PRs on the track. Joshua Gamel (16:59, 4:29) could make a major jump this season.

That’s not to say their No. 4 and No. 5 runners aren't good. Jackson Alderman (17:42) and Benjamin Scanga (17:44) both return to anchor this squad.

We're excited to see what they can do against the other teams in the Dual County League, especially previously mentioned Newton South.

Wellesley

We talked a bit about this team in the Bay State Conference article, but we never went fully in-depth on them.

Daniel Goldberg returns as a solid front-runner for the squad after running a 16:04 5K last year at the Bay State Invitational.

He's backed up by Nate Flynn (16:44, 4:44) and Solomon Jin (17:12, 4:43).

After that, Conor Foley (17:25) and Peter Mahoney (17:37) round out this squad. Keep an eye on Mahoney, as he's coming off a big two-mile PR of 10:21.

We also touched on this in the Bay State Conference article, but we’re excited to see where Wellesley falls against previously mentioned Needham and Walpole.
