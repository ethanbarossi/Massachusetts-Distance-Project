Week 6 Power Rankings

Cambridge makes its long-awaited debut, BC High surges back into the top 10, and the divisional races are heating up.

By Ethan Barossi  ·  October 19, 2026

BC High team after a big fourth-place finish at the Burnt Hills Invitational.

After the cancellation of the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational, there were still plenty of questions surrounding where certain teams stood heading into the postseason. Fortunately, the Catholic Memorial Invitational gave us some much-needed answers.

With several teams making major statements, others struggling to find consistency, and a few surprising performances shaking up the rankings, Week 6 brought some significant movement.

Let’s break down the biggest changes and which teams could be at the top when the postseason arrives.

Course comparison note: For comparisons with the Bay State Invitational, we estimate that subtracting roughly 7–11 seconds from Catholic Memorial Invitational times at Franklin Park provides a more accurate comparison between the two races. This is an estimate rather than an exact course conversion.

#1 Cambridge Rindge & Latin

The wait is finally over.

The top-ranked team in Massachusetts finally made its season debut on Saturday, and Cambridge Rindge & Latin looks even stronger than many expected.

Leon Ibanez-Fraile led the squad, taking the individual victory in 15:37. Sophomore Thomas DeWitt followed closely behind, placing fifth in 15:43.

This duo brings some serious star power to Cambridge and has the ability to compete with the best individuals in the state. When the state championship arrives in November, the potential battle between Ibanez-Fraile and DeWitt against Brookline’s Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem and Liam Hartmann could be one of the most exciting matchups of the season.

Liam Eldridge was next across the line in 15:49, taking seven seconds off his personal best from the 2025 season.

Gael Berzin (16:07) and Henry Parker (16:09) rounded out the scoring five, and both had impressive performances of their own. Berzin dropped an incredible 40 seconds from his previous best, while Parker improved by 44 seconds.

Last season, it was clear that Cambridge had an outstanding group of four runners. However, Berzin and Parker, who were around the fifth and sixth positions at the time, were noticeably farther behind. Both have clearly taken major steps forward and are now giving Cambridge the depth needed to compete for a state championship.

Perhaps the most impressive part of this performance is that Cambridge was missing one of its top returning runners, Kieran Eichler. Eichler entered the season with a 16:03 personal best but did not compete on Saturday. If he returns to form, Cambridge could become even more dangerous.

With #2 Brookline chasing its fifth consecutive state title, one question remains: What can Brookline do to respond to this performance?

#3 Concord-Carlisle

Talk about a comeback.

When Concord-Carlisle competed at the Bay State Invitational a few weeks ago, the team struggled, leaving us questioning whether this would be its year. Saturday’s performance at the Catholic Memorial Invitational proved those doubts wrong.

Despite losing some major senior talent from last season, Concord-Carlisle has once again established itself as one of the top teams in Massachusetts. Six of its top seven runners recorded personal bests, even on a more challenging course.

Braeden Quayle led the squad with an impressive 11th-place finish in 15:49. For perspective, that was only nine seconds behind Newton North’s Ryan Costello and six seconds behind Cambridge’s Thomas DeWitt.

Toby Martel followed with a 16:04, improving his personal best by six seconds and separating himself from the rest of Concord’s pack.

Then came the depth.

Sawyer Bout led the next group in 16:19, followed almost immediately by Will Okie in 16:20. Ryan Perry (16:22) and Jack Thomas (16:22.4) rounded out an incredibly tight group.

Even with the more difficult conditions, Concord-Carlisle’s pack improved by roughly 10 seconds across the board. This team is getting better quickly, and the timing couldn’t be much better.

The performance also makes the upcoming Division 1B championship even more interesting. With all the movement in the rankings over the past few weeks, the division has become much more competitive.

North Andover and Concord-Carlisle now appear headed toward an extremely close battle for the title. Can Concord-Carlisle’s depth overcome North Andover’s top-end talent?

#9 BC High

With the return of its top runner, BC High makes one of the biggest jumps of the week, skyrocketing from #20 to #9.

Colin Kurtz is back.

After an extended absence during the middle of the season, Kurtz returned with an outstanding performance at the Burnt Hills Invitational. He finished seventh overall in 15:19 over 3.05 miles, which is roughly equivalent to a 15:45 5K.

His absence had made it difficult to evaluate BC High against the state’s top teams. Now, with Kurtz back in the lineup, the Eagles are firmly back in the conversation.

Joseph Hayes followed closely behind, placing 10th overall in 15:31. Hayes has had a relatively quiet season, but this performance, roughly equivalent to a 16:00 5K, gives us more confidence in what he can contribute moving forward.

Nolan Kurtz (15:58) and Brendon Donovan (16:03) were the next two runners across the line. Nolan Kurtz, in particular, has put together an impressive season, taking on a larger scoring role during his brother’s absence.

Their fifth runner, Terrance McGhee Jr., finished in 16:30, while Theo Weiss had a more difficult outing.

Weiss entered the season with a 16:02 personal best, and if he can return to that level, BC High could have another important piece of its scoring lineup.

With Colin Kurtz back and the rest of the team continuing to develop, the Eagles are looking much more like the squad we expected entering the season.

#11 Weston and #12 Arlington

Both Weston and Arlington move ahead of Reading Memorial and Natick this week as we finally get a clearer picture of where these teams stand.

One of the biggest reasons for the movement was Arlington’s upset victory over Reading.

Although Max King dropped out of another race, Reading’s inconsistency throughout the season is becoming increasingly difficult to overlook. We still believe Reading has a stronger lineup than Arlington when everyone is performing at their best. The problem is that Reading has yet to consistently show that strength.

Natick finds itself in a similar position. The team hasn’t shown much improvement over the past month, and its performance at the Catholic Memorial Invitational only raised more questions.

Natick’s top five finished as follows: Declan Hava (15:50), Callum Doyle (16:13), Benjamin Gidelson (16:27), Alex Forte (16:47), and Dylan Cooke (16:49).

While the team still has plenty of talent, it hasn’t taken the step forward we expected at this point in the season.

Arlington, on the other hand, stepped up when it mattered. Owen Merrill (16:01), Zachary Weinberg (16:08), and Evan Jacobson (16:34) all recorded personal bests and contributed important points for the squad.

Eli Hanover also played a major role, running 16:18 and finishing fifth overall in the team’s matchup.

This Arlington squad isn’t finished yet. The team still has a major test against Woburn this week, but a victory would give Arlington the opportunity to finish 6-0 in Middlesex League competition.

Although Arlington remains slightly behind the top contenders in Division 1B, its recent performances suggest that it could be a team to watch when the postseason arrives.

#15 Longmeadow

Longmeadow wants a piece of the Division 2 championship conversation.

After a big victory over Amherst-Pelham, the team moves up several positions in this week’s rankings. Although Longmeadow recorded only one personal best, it was a significant one.

Jacob Abebe absolutely shattered his previous season best of 16:37, running a new personal best of 15:45.

Abebe dealt with injuries during parts of last fall, leaving some uncertainty about how much he could contribute this season. Saturday’s performance answered those questions in a big way.

He dominated the race and finished 24 seconds ahead of teammate CJ Deliso, who ran 16:09.

With Longmeadow climbing to #15, the team has officially entered the Division 2 conversation. Groton-Dunstable and Weston remain the top contenders, but Longmeadow is beginning to look like a serious challenger.

After falling short of expectations last season, this team has plenty to prove. Can Longmeadow reclaim its divisional title?

#19 Oliver Ames

After several controlled efforts earlier in the season, Oliver Ames finally showed what it could do at full strength.

The team went all out in its dual meet against Mansfield, delivering a performance strong enough to move it from unranked to #19.

As expected, Devon McStowe led the squad, recording a new personal best of 15:46.

We had also predicted that Corey Yifru had the potential to run alongside McStowe, and he certainly delivered. Yifru finished second in the dual meet with an outstanding time of 15:53, giving Oliver Ames a dangerous front-running duo.

Jake Hauvuy followed with a season best of 16:32 at the Catholic Memorial Invitational.

Interestingly, Hauvuy had been beaten earlier in the week by teammate Peter Feodoroff, who ran a personal best of 16:37 in the dual meet.

Nicholas Colvin rounded out the scoring group in 16:59, helping provide depth while Colin Donahue was absent.

Oliver Ames’ emergence has made the Division 1C championship race considerably more interesting.

Between Reading’s inconsistency, Billerica’s outstanding top-end talent, and Oliver Ames’ recent improvements, this division could be even more competitive than it was last year.

Earlier in the season, Reading looked like the clear favorite to run away with the title. Now, Oliver Ames is showing that it has a legitimate opportunity to defend its championship.

With both teams preparing for what could be another major postseason showdown, Division 1C is shaping up to be one of the most exciting divisional battles in Massachusetts.
