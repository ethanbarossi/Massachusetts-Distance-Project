2026 Division 3: What to Expect

Division 3 is quite different from Divisions 1 and 2. Most of the talent actually comes out of the smallest division, 3C, where both of next week’s likely podium teams compete. Still, there’s plenty of talent throughout all three divisions that deserves to be talked about.

Division 3A

Team Seedings

  1. Greater Lowell Tech
  2. East Bridgewater
  3. Quabbin Regional
  4. Belchertown

Greater Lowell Tech has the slight edge here, but you can expect East Bridgewater to give them a run for their money. Greater Lowell currently has five of the top 20 seeds, as well as Mathew Guerin, who is currently seeded in the top five.

East Bridgewater should definitely be challenging them, though. They have more top-end strength than Greater Lowell but less depth. They currently have three runners seeded to make the podium, while Greater Lowell only has two. However, their No. 4 and No. 5 runners are currently back in the 25–30 range. If East Bridgewater wants a shot at winning this meet, those runners will need to move up.

Key Individuals

  • Harrison Mello — Hudson
  • Cassidy McBee — Triton Regional
  • Cohen Zahn — Pittsfield
  • Mathew Guerin — Greater Lowell Tech
  • Ethan Levesque — Greater Lowell Tech
  • Tommy Rudha — East Bridgewater
  • Benjamin Levesque — East Bridgewater

The main battle up front seems to be between Harrison Mello and Cassidy McBee. We’d give Mello the edge, as he actually beat McBee earlier in the season at the Twilight Invitational. Mello then lowered his PR even further at his conference meet, running 15:52, something McBee has yet to do.

However, seeds four through seven are all from Greater Lowell or East Bridgewater, so expect a tight battle behind these two runners.

Division 3B

Team Seedings

  1. Littleton
  2. Hampshire Regional
  3. Amesbury

Littleton definitely looks like the favorite to take home the title here. They’re a strong team that really doesn’t get enough recognition. Adrian Ioan and Maxwell Thompson lead the squad up front, with four runners seeded to make the podium. We’d be very surprised if this team doesn’t run away with the title.

Hampshire Regional also looks like a comfortable second-place pick. They’re led by two podium contenders in Hobbes O’Sullivan and Owen Cubi, with three more runners seeded around 25th. This team is a bit farther back from Littleton but comfortably ahead of Amesbury.

Key Individuals

  • Evan Hedlund — Frontier
  • Matthew Pilon — Lunenburg
  • Sokari Jenkins — Gardner
  • Adrian Ioan — Littleton
  • Maxwell Thompson — Littleton

Although Evan Hedlund has been having a somewhat underwhelming season, he should still be the favorite to take home the title. He enters the race as the No. 1 seed and has been known to run more conservatively during the first half of his races, something particularly useful at Northfield Mountain.

Another thing about Northfield Mountain is that the course can favor runners with longer strides. We wouldn’t be completely shocked if Matthew Pilon took home the title, as his taller stature could give him an advantage.

Sokari Jenkins is also seeded just behind Pilon, so he should be right up there with the lead group as well.

Don’t sleep on Maxwell Thompson, though. He hasn’t PR’d yet this season but could be a real threat if he gets back to his previous level. Thompson currently has a season best of 16:08 and a PR of 15:59.

Division 3C

Team Seedings

  1. Hopedale
  2. Mt. Greylock Regional
  3. Bromfield
  4. Parker Charter Essential

Hopedale looks to be the clear favorite here. They currently have four podium contenders and are the only ranked Division 3 team in the state’s Top 20. Quinn Cook leads the squad up front, with Ben Stone, Connor Fitzgibbon, and Cedric Arthur all looking to take home some hardware.

The main battle appears to be for second place between the other three teams. Although Parker Charter looks like it could be the No. 2 seed, we know they’ve been dealing with multiple injuries throughout the season. These issues have been particularly noticeable with Cypress Lance and Zephyr Schermerhorn, leaving one of their scoring five runners around the 17:30 mark. Still, we wouldn’t completely count them out just yet.

Mt. Greylock barely edges out Bromfield for second in our seedings, though it’s going to be an extremely tight battle. Greylock’s biggest strength is its depth. They currently have five of the top 25 seeds, with their No. 5 runner coming in ahead of Bromfield’s No. 3.

Bromfield’s strength, however, is something Greylock doesn’t have: top-end talent. Bromfield is led by Kai Kemeza and Ben McWaters, who are currently seeded fifth and sixth overall. Greylock’s top runner, Patrick Holland, is currently seeded ninth.

Key Individuals

  • Quinn Cook — Hopedale
  • Ben Stone — Hopedale
  • Trevor Sullivan — Granby
  • Konrad Schluter — Maynard
  • Jett Johnson — Parker Charter
  • Kai Kemeza — Bromfield
  • Ben McWaters — Bromfield

This looks like a four-way battle between Cook, Schluter, Sullivan, and Johnson, though Cook definitely has the edge. Already being the No. 1 runner in the field, combined with his tall stature, should give Cook an advantage on the challenging terrain of Northfield Mountain.

Trevor Sullivan enters as the No. 2 seed, though he does lack some height compared to the other competitors. Sullivan will most likely perform better at the Meet of Champions than at Northfield Mountain, but don’t count him out of anything.

Konrad Schluter and Jett Johnson come in next. Schluter is usually the type of runner who likes to take the lead early, but that strategy almost certainly won’t work on a course like this. If Schluter can pace himself more effectively, he’ll have a much better shot at placing highly.

Johnson should also be right up there after finishing less than 10 seconds behind Cook at their conference meet.
