Divisional Drama: Weekend in Review

What a weekend. Upsets, big moments, and races that came down to the final stretch. Here we’ll break down the best moments that happened this weekend and which teams walked away making the biggest statements.

Division 1A

Like we said, they didn’t need to be the best team in the state, just the best team on the day. Still, after looking at their performance it’s hard to put into words what Brookline accomplished.

They beat Cambridge 61-79 to place 4 runners inside the top 20. Liam Hartmann (15:44) led the way with a runner-up finish, followed only 6 seconds behind was Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem (15:50) who finished 5th overall.

Brookline’s next 2 scorers played a pivotal role in their win. Elias Thomasson (15:59) and Matt Ritter (16:19) both put on some of the best performances of the day. We’ve been watching these runners grow over the course of the season, but when their team needed them most they made the biggest statement. Thomasson truly stepped up for his team to grab a 9th place finish, even when he wasn’t even seeded to make the podium. Thomasson's performance is also highlighted by him taking big individual wins over key runners like Lexington’s Trevor Stevens and Weymouth’s Patrick Geagan. Ritter came in next for the Warriors to just miss the podium and place 16th overall. Ritter had a great day where he outkicked Cambridge’s #3 runner and beat some of the state’s top individuals.

Nam Danh rounded out the Brookline squad to place 29th overall. He was most likely a bit farther back than they hoped but it’s also important to note that Brookline was sitting their actual #5 runner in Kieran Morrissey-Dhenke. It’s likely to assume that the Brookline coaching staff just didn’t want to race the star freshman 2 weeks in a row. We're confident he’ll be back running for the Warriors next weekend.

Cambridge finished 2nd overall and they had a few more problems than we anticipated. Leon Ibanez-Fraile (16:28) and Liam Eldridge (16:26) both did not have their best day which was a big contributor to their loss. Still they put up some great performances specifically in Thomas Dewitt (15:48) who finished 4th overall to outkick Abdel-Dayem, and Gael Berzin (16:15) who made the podium to place 14th overall.

Lexington then followed with an exciting performance to place 3rd overall. We actually thought this team was the 5th seed coming in but they had a great day to defeat BC High, Newton North, and St. John’s Prep.

This squad was led by Patrick Noonan (15:48) who placed 3rd overall. This is a great race for Noonan as it shows he’s ready to compete with the best runners in the state, losing only to Hartmann and Zoss. Trevor Stevens (16:01) came in next for the squad to place 10th overall in another great individual performance. Andrew Zhang (16:46) then finally came back into form to place 35th overall and anchor this squad.

On the individual side there’s also a lot to talk about.

Landon Zoss (15:39), who was the meet’s #1 returner truly felt that weight on his shoulders. In a very inspiring race he outkicked Hartmann in the closing stretches to claim the title for D1A Champion. This time also makes Zoss the #1 seed for next week’s Meet of Champions.

Ryan Costello (15:50) led Newton North to finish 6th overall individually. After his performance at his conference meet, he looks to be getting to be a big podium contender for next week.

Colin Kurtz (15:53.0), the catholic conference champion, and Lowell’s Sam De Souza (15:53.1) came in next to place 7th and 8th overall. Both put up great performances and get very excited about what they can accomplish next weekend.

St. John’s Holden Hallee (16:14) came into place 12th overall just behind Weymouth’s Patrick Geagan (16:04). For Geagan he appeared to be pacing most of the race but fell off a bit towards the third lap. Though for Hallee this is a big race for him. After coming off a win in the large school junior race last weekend, he accomplished his goals of making the podium and qualifying for the Meet of Champions.

Newton South’s Fran Tibau Martorell (16:18), who won the large senior race last weekend, took the last spot on the podium to place 15th overall. He had a great race to lead his team to 7th overall and qualify with his teammates to the Meet of Champions.

Also big shoutout to Lowell and Andover who both managed to qualify for the All-State meet next weekend, despite not placing inside the top 7.

Division 1B

Teamwork. That's what separated D1B yesterday. Although it was supposed to be a 2 team battle, the top 5 teams were all separated by 20 points and neither of the 2 teams took home the gold trophy.

Lincoln-Sudbury put together the best performance of the weekend. Every time you looked at that race you saw 3 jerseys up at the front. All 3 worked off each other, and all 3 did it together. This all came together specifically for Josh Gamel (16:03) who took home the individual title and upset all the favorites. Brandon Ma (16:12.8) and Lukas Masiakos (16:12.9) also showed from friendly competition to cross the line together for 6th and 7th overall.

We knew this team would have a big trio up front, but we didn’t expect their back-end to come through and challenge for the win. We were very mistaken.

Tasker Mendelson (16:54) had a big day to place 30th overall followed by Ben Scanga in 47th. These 2 runners came through for their team during the right race. This all contributed to a big 91-102 win over #1 seed Concord-Carlisle. The Lincoln-Sudbury boys put one of the best performances from a team all season, Congratulations!

Though at Massachusetts Distance Project, there’s a reason we don’t only talk about the winning team and individual. Everyone has their own stories to tell.

This couldn’t be more true for Concord-Carlisle. Although they only finished runner-up it was still an amazing performance. We knew Jackson Misek would be out the whole season, but their current #1 runner, Braeden Quayle, also didn’t step on the line. That's right, Concord just placed 2nd overall without their top 2 runners. Clearly they are still one of the deepest teams in the state.

The team had an impressive 36-second spread from 1-5. Toby Martel (16:23) led the squad to place 12th overall. Will Okie (16:30) who was coming off a great race at his conference meet, continued his momentum to place 17th overall. Ryan Perry (16:36), Sawyer Bout (16:38) and Jack Thomas (16:59) rounded out the squad. It’s important to note that their pack was very tight throughout the whole race, when you saw one Concord runner, you saw the next. Even if it wasn’t the result they wanted, this is still one of the best performances of the day.

Arlington came in next for third. They had a great day to then beat out North Andover who was the meet’s #2 seed. Arlington notched them out by 1 point (108-109) Owen Merrill (16:11) had a great day to lead his squad and place 4th overall. Zach Weinberg (16:24) and Eli Hanover (16:28) followed behind to place 14th and 16th overall. This team had been a bit under our radar all season. They haven’t run any invitationals so it was always hard to understand where they really stood. Though now we can confidently say that they are one of the state's top teams and are not to be underestimated.

Although North Andover wasn’t out any of their top runners, it’s important to note that we know they had some sickness issues in one of their top runners. We are definitely under the assumption that when everyone is healthy, the battle between CC, NA, and LS is going to be a lot closer.

Liam Rand (16:24) and Tucker LaRosa (16:28) led the squad to just make the podium finishing 13th and 15th overall. Tristan Rand then finished 21st, Owen Desmand finished 17th and Will Thomas rounded out the squad in 33rd overall. We definitely still have high hopes for North Andover and think they can run a lot faster.

Natick was only 2 points behind and was definitely a big surprise. They scored 111 points to place 5th overall. Callum Doyle had a day to lead the squad. He really hasn’t been having that good of a season with underwhelming performances at the Catholic Memorial Invitational and Bay State Conference Championship. Though on Saturday he had a big upset to take third overall in 16:11. Declan Hava (16:14) then followed closely behind for 9th overall. Ben Gidelson (16:41), Dylan Cooke (17:02), and Alex Forte (17:03) rounded out the squad.

Belmont came in for 6th overall and their trio had a great day. Ishaan Kanagaraj had a great day to place 5th overall and lead the squad. Coming off the hill he was in 2nd place which was a big shock to us. He’s usually the slowest member of the trio but put up a very good performance. Will Olmstead (16:15) and Noah Geiger (16:19) also made the podium to finish 10th and 11th respectively.

Xaverian rounded out the top 7 teams. They didn’t have their best day and it truly showed. Luckily they made the cutoff for next week and have one more chance to redeem themselves. Jack Weintraub (16:05) led the squad to finish 2nd overall.

Division 1C

The Reading Memorial boys have been a team that have been pretty lackluster all season. We knew this team had their eyes on this race from the beginning of the season, though as it went on their chances of winning kept decreasing. Though over these past few weeks this team has managed to find their way back. In an absolute thriller of a race Reading reclaims their D1C title 66-93 over Oliver Ames. This is sweet revenge from last year after Reading’s 4 point loss to OA.

4 rockets made the podium last year. This year even being without all 4 of those same runners, they put on a more impressive feat. Finn Johnson-Houlihan (16:32) led the squad to place 6th individually. This was definitely a very big moment for Johnson-Houlihan, last year he was one of the team’s top runners but had to wrap his season up early due to injury. From the sidelines he watched his team lose a big race that he knows they could’ve won if he was able to race. This year, even while dealing with injury issues earlier in the season,  he was able to lead his team to taking back that D1C trophy.

Declan Grant (16:36) finished right behind Johnson-Houlihan for 7th overall. Grant has been having an amazing season that all lead to this moment. With some of his senior teammates out due to injury, Grant knew he had to step up. Over the course of the season Grant had gradually been climbing the depth chart and put on a great performance to help his team.

Colin Carroll (16:44) and Colin Herlihy (16:45) also managed to make the podium for the rockets, placing 12th and 13th overall. It was really a big day for both runners, as they knew they had their team leaning on their shoulders. Working as a pack, Reading moved through the race, pushing both of these runners to go as fast as they could. Curran Healy (17:11) rounded out the squad for a 28th place finish.

On the individual side the front runners were already separated by the first lap. Working as teammates Billerica had a huge 1-2 individual finish by Mason Niles (15:47) and Sahil Gandhi (15:49). Working as teammates gives them the strength that other teams don’t have. Anthony Malakidis (15:50) who finished third overall tried to challenge the duo, but both teammates just knew they had to put it all out there for each other.

Division 2A

No surprise over here, Longmeadow destroyed the competition. Longmeadow placed 4 runners inside the top 10 and scored just 42 points. Jacob Abebe led the squad to take the individual win in 15:30. CJ Deliso (15:53) finished 4th overall for the squad in what looks to be a conservative effort. The team knew they were going to win so it seems that Deliso was pacing some of his teammates for the first bit of the race. Aidan Kretschmar then had a big day to place 7th overall in a PR of 16:12, while Henry Cournoyer placed 10th in 16:35. This team’s performance shows why we have them as the #1 seed for D2 and they should be looking for a lot more going into next week.

In our predictions we mentioned that 2nd place would be tight between Boston Latin Academy, Walpole, and Masconomet, though we were unsure about Masconomet because they hadn’t put down any seed times. They proceeded to show us that they didn’t need any.

Alexander Hamlin had a great day to lead the squad to place 3rd overall in a PR of 15:49. Three more of his teammates then placed inside the top 20 to dip under 17:00. Jonah Seffens (16:52) placed 16th, Conall Strangman (16:56) followed in 17th, while Millan Ladd also ran 16:56 for 18th. This team took home a big 2nd place finish with 75 points. Boston Latin Academy placed 3rd with 91 and Walpole was 4th with 120.

Though BLA still had a great day from their top 4. All four runners placed inside the top 8 with 2 PR’s. Bradon Spiess led the squad with a runner-up finish in 15:37, followed by Adam Kramer (15:54) and Nate Iliff (15:59) in 5th and 6th. With Khalid Jama placing 8th in 16:13.

Division 2B

Groton Dunstable (78) took care of business to defeat Amherst-Pelham (101) and Northampton (112). In a very individually stacked division, Groton managed to place three on the podium and have a big 3-4 finish.

Andrew Kosiba (15:49.1) led the squad followed almost immediately by Greyson Duane (15:49.3). We stated that Duane was definitely a runner to watch, he hadn’t put down a good seed time yet, but we knew he was very fast.

Micah Pierantozzi then ran 16:13 to place 12th overall and have a third runner on the podium. Ashton Duane (16:26) and Ashvik Yadav (17:20) rounded out the squad. It’s also important to note that Cameron Duane is usually a key member of the squad but was #7 on Saturday. With Duane at full health, Groton can score even less points.

Amherst-Pelham took runner-up in this race highlighted by Calvin Miller’s 2nd place finish in 15:48, and Peter Nedeau placing 10th in 16:09.

Notably Northampton was actually a lot closer to Amherst than we anticipated. The squad had a great day with 4 runners under 17:00, and five runners in the top 32. Shea O’Neil specifically had a big day to lead the squad in a 6th place finish of 16:02.

Though the individual side is definitely one of the most interesting aspects of D2B. In an amazing race East Longmeadow’s Nick Rivera took the individual title in a time of 15:40. This was a big win for Rivera as we predicted he’d be around the 3rd or 4th seed.

With Nate Palmieri not having his best day, teammate Logan Holloway stepped up for his team. Holloway ran an impressive PR of 15:51 to place 5th overall. This performance helped Pembroke with a 5th place finish, securing a position at the Meet of Champions.

Ashland’s Luca Domestico (16:05), Dracut’s Tyler Mogavero (16:06), Wayland’s Brandon Hopps (16:09), and Newburyport’s Nordeen Fadil (16:11) were also some of the meet’s top finishers.

Division 2C

Again Weston continues to impress. This team won with just 47 points, went 1-2 in the race, another contender in the top 10, and all five runners inside the top 20. On top of all of this, they were without their #4 runner.

Mathew Fitzgibbon led the squad to take the individual honors in 15:49. This was a great win for Fitzgibbon as he beat teammate Hunter Waechter (15:56). We also know that Fitzgibbon has had a bit of an injury heavy season, so he’s definitely peaking at the right time.

Freshman Jonah Watkins finished 7th overall for the squad in a PR of 16:15. We know Watkins is one of the state’s top freshmen and he proved it by his performance on Saturday.

Aarav Mehta (16:44), and Kevin Han (16:46) rounded out the squad for 18th in 19th overall. Mehta is a clear standout, significantly taking down his previous SB.

Longmeadow, Groton, and Weston will all face off next week for the Division 2 title.

Notably, Swampscott upset Whitinsville Christian 112-119. The team had a great day with two finishers inside the top six. Atticus Jakious (16:10) led the squad to place 4th, while Anthony Colella (16:14) placed 6th overall. We thought that Whitinsville was the clear #2 seed, so Swampscott’s upset is very impressive. Although this team wasn’t on our radar, we now have high hopes for them going into next week.

Although Whitinsville’s performance was a bit upsetting, Ben Baron (16:08) still had a big day to lead his team placing 3rd individually.

Division 3A

Unpredictable. That's the only word to describe the treacherous terrain of Northfield Mountain.

Division 3 already started off with one of the biggest battles of the day. Greater Lowell Tech barely edged out East Bridgewater 82-84. This battle was so tight and came down to multiple matchups.

First Greater Lowell’s Ethan Levesque (17:07) barely outmatched Bridgewater’s Benjamin Levesque (17:10) for a 5th and 6th place finish. Already the teams were battling it out. Bridgewater’s Ethan Rhuda then came in for 9th while Greater Lowell responded with Mathew Guerin outkicking Bridgewater’s Gabe Cook for 11th and 12th.

Greater Lowell’s Patrick Sullivan took the last spot on the podium for both teams to each have 3 medalists. Though at the end of the day it is Greater Lowell’s depth. Timothy Sullivan came in for 22nd while Matteo Dos Santos came in for 29th.

On the individual side Harrison Mello again got the better of Cassidy McBee. Mello won in 16:33 to McBee’s 16:35.

Division 3B

Littleton boys showed why we have them as one of the top contenders. This team put together an amazing performance to score just 61 points with 4 runners on the podium. Adrian Ioan (16:49) and Max Thompson (16:54) led the squad with a 4-5 finish. A pair of brothers, Pranav and Pranshu Sharma then went on to place 12th and 14th respectively. This team put together an amazing performance, especially considering the harsh conditions of Northfield Mountain.

Hampshire Regional then barely edged Amesbury 92-100. Hampshire’s performance was shown through Owen Cubi (17:13) and Hobbes O’Sullivan (17:25) placing 8th and 11th overall with 3 more runners inside the top 30.

On the individual side Evan Hedlund took control. He hasn’t had his best season so far but really turned it around this weekend. He ran a time of 16:02 which was the fastest time across all three Division 3 races. He was 16 seconds ahead of second place and is showing that he will be someone to watch heading into next week.

Matthew Pilon (16:18) and Sokari Jenkins (16:26) also put on impressive performances to place 2nd and 3rd overall.

Division 3C

The unpredictable statement couldn’t be more true for D3C. As expected Hopedale took the overall win but not without some surprises.

Ben Stone actually led the squad this weekend with an individual runner-up finish in 16:30. Connor Fitzgibbon was also closely behind to place 5th overall in 16:33. Although Quinn Cook (16:47) didn’t have his best day to finish 10th, the rest of his team stepped up.

Cedric Arthur also made the podium to finish 14th overall in 16:55. Jack Murphy then had a great day to finish 17th overall in 17:11. Usually Murphy’s been a bit farther back than the rest of the runners so it was nice seeing him a bit closer to their #4. Ethan Dewolf then got in front of 2nd place Parker Charter’s #5 runner to support as the 6th man.

Parker Charter still had a big day, even without one of their top runners. It was a tight battle for 2nd though we did think they were the #4 seed.

Zephyr Schermerhorn came out of left field to take the individual state title. We didn’t even have him in our runners to watch. He ran a time of 16:27 to outlast Stone from Hopedale. He’s also had a bit of an injury heavy season, sitting out the twilight meet and his conference meet, just reiterating how surprising his win was. Jett Johnson then came in to place 6th overall in 16:40, while Nathan Davis and Danny Garrison placed 20th and 21st. Although we didn’t think that highly of this team before, they should definitely be the favorite for the runner-up trophy next weekend.

There were also some great individual performances with Lenox’s Ben Harewood placing 3rd in 16:31, Ayer Shirley’s Jake Leone following right behind in 16:31.4 for 4th overall. Ben McWaters led the Bromfield team to place 7th in 16:44, and Granby’s Trevor Sullivan placed 8th in 16:46.
