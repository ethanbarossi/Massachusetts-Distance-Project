2026 Meet of Champions Predictions

With only one meet left in the season, championship season is truly coming into full swing. Although each division has a clear favorite, there are plenty of teams capable of making huge statements. We'll break down who we think will step up one last time this season.

Division 1

The clear favorite here is Brookline, and there's a reason we moved them to the #1 spot in our rankings before the D1A meet even happened. We know their ability to perform in the postseason, and they've proven it time and time again. In the past, this team has pulled off key upsets against teams like BC High and Concord-Carlisle, and we believe Cambridge is just the next team in line.

Last year, their performance at the Meet of Champions was highlighted by two runners. As the postseason started to heat up, both Harry Flint and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem put together breakout performances, resulting in Flint placing 16th at the MOC and Abdel-Dayem finishing 19th.

This year, they once again have two runners coming off breakout races last weekend who will look to carry that momentum into Saturday. Elias Thomasson had a great day, placing 9th overall, while Matt Ritter finished 16th. With Liam Hartmann and Abdel-Dayem leading from the front, these four runners will definitely pack a punch.

It was also clear last weekend that Brookline was at a bit of a disadvantage, as they sat their #5 runner. However, Kieran Morrissey-Dhenke should be back in the lineup this week, serving as a strong anchor for the squad.

Looking at Cambridge Rindge & Latin, it's hard to tell what kind of performance this team will put together. Last weekend was definitely not their best showing, but the question remains: How well can this team perform in the postseason?

Kieran Eichler should be a bright spot heading into Saturday. He placed 18th overall last weekend in a time of 16:20, and that was his season opener. Eichler should be looking to climb the standings this week and hopefully finish alongside some of his teammates.

Liam Eldridge and Leon Ibanez-Fraile will also need to bounce back from last weekend. They're usually the squad's #1 and #3 runners, but on Saturday, they rounded out the scoring as #4 and #5. Both runners should be aiming for the podium this weekend, with Ibanez-Fraile having the ability to contend for a top-five finish.

Lexington isn't far behind Cambridge and has the best shot at pulling off an upset for a trophy. Patrick Noonan and Trevor Stevens lead this squad and should both be aiming for the podium. Alden Hall didn't have his best race last weekend, but if he can bounce back, those points will be extremely valuable. Rhys Behlings and Andrew Zhang round out the squad.

Out of these four, Zoss is our clear favorite. He put together a great race last weekend to become the #1 seed heading into this meet. If Zoss can once again stay calm and patient throughout the race before taking control in the closing stretch, he has the ability to take home the title.

However, Mason Niles should be a serious threat. These two have gone back and forth throughout the season and are currently 1-1 against each other. There's a very good chance the battle for the state championship will come down to these two facing off one last time.

Division 2

Longmeadow once again finds itself in position to take home a state title. Although they've been in this situation before, we're confident this squad has learned from its past mistakes.

Based on their performances at the conference and divisional championships, Longmeadow has skyrocketed to #4 in our power rankings, sitting behind only the three Division 1 teams previously mentioned. The gap is so large that the next Division 2 team, Weston, doesn't appear until #12.

Last year, Longmeadow came into this meet with a real shot at the title but ended up losing by just one point to Boston Latin Academy after one of its runners passed out in the middle of the course. They also had a similar opportunity to win a state title in the spring, but a disqualification dropped them to third, leaving them without a trophy.

We're confident this will finally be the day Longmeadow gets its revenge.

CJ Deliso and Jacob Abebe lead this squad from the front, and both are in contention for the individual title. Abebe enters as the #1 seed based on last weekend's results, while Deliso comes in with the faster overall PR of 15:18.

Finn O'Connor is another key member of the squad, entering with a PR of 15:51. O'Connor was also the state runner-up in the pentathlon last year, showing off his impressive range and athleticism. Aidan Kretschmar and Henry Cournoyer round out the squad, with both placing inside the top 10 at last weekend's Division 2A Championship.

The main battle should be for the runner-up trophy between #12 Weston and #14 Groton-Dunstable. Both teams put together stellar performances last weekend, with Weston having a slightly stronger showing.

We'd give Weston the edge, but not by much. There should be a great battle up front between Weston's Hunter Waechter and Matthew Fitzgibbon and Groton-Dunstable's Andrew Kosiba and Greyson Duane. All four are great runners and should be aiming for the podium.

This should be an amazing race to watch, especially since these teams didn't face each other last weekend. Longmeadow took the crown in D2A, Groton-Dunstable won D2B, and Weston stole the show in D2C. We're excited to see how these teams match up when they finally go head-to-head.

Division 2 also has an incredibly strong individual field, making it difficult to predict who will come out on top. Our two favorites are Longmeadow's Abebe and Deliso, but there are plenty of runners capable of taking the title.

The previously mentioned Kosiba, Duane, Waechter, and Fitzgibbon are all in contention. Other major names include Bradon Spiess and Adam Kramer from Boston Latin Academy, East Longmeadow's Nick Rivera, who captured the D2B crown, and Amherst's Calvin Miller, who finished runner-up in D2B right behind Rivera.

Nate Palmieri from Pembroke and Zachary Mazzaferro from East Longmeadow should also be in contention. Neither had their best race last weekend, but we can definitely see both bouncing back with big performances.

Division 3

Hopedale will look to close out its stellar season by taking home the Division 3 state title. Led by a strong quartet up front, they're clearly the team to beat.

Quinn Cook leads this squad and should also be a major contender for the individual title. He enters as the #1 seed with a PR of 15:15, ranking #2 in Massachusetts.

He's backed up by Ben Stone, who put together an incredible performance at last year's Meet of Champions, placing 4th overall and becoming the meet's #3 returner. Stone also recently took runner-up honors at the D3C Championship at Northfield Mountain. Definitely keep an eye on him this weekend.

Connor Fitzgibbon and Cedric Arthur also come in as strong front runners and should be looking to contend for podium spots.

Parker Charter appears to be a step behind Hopedale but remains a strong contender for the runner-up trophy.

Jett Johnson and Zephyr Schermerhorn lead Parker Charter from the front. Johnson is definitely a top-five contender, entering the meet with a PR of 15:38. Schermerhorn recently put together the best performance of his career, taking the D3C individual crown on the difficult terrain of Northfield Mountain. He finished just eight seconds off his PR on that course, so it's safe to say he's capable of running much faster. Danny Garrison and Nathan Davis are also major contributors to this squad.

We doubt Parker Charter will go down without a fight. The three-time defending state champions have something to prove this year, even after dealing with an injury-heavy season. Although Hopedale appears to have separated itself from the field, we wouldn't be surprised if Parker Charter had a few tricks up its sleeve.

Cook is definitely the favorite heading into this race. He's had a spectacular fall season and, before last weekend, was undefeated against Massachusetts competition. However, he shouldn't expect to go without a challenge.

Evan Hedlund is the meet's #1 returner from last year and is definitely someone to watch. Hedlund hadn't been having his best season, but he completely turned things around last weekend. On the difficult terrain of Northfield Mountain, Hedlund captured the D3B title, beating every other runner in the division by at least 16 seconds. Do not sleep on him heading into Saturday.

Trevor Sullivan is a heck of a runner, too. He's put together several notable performances this season. Back in September, he beat Hartmann at the Bay State Invitational and has actually beaten Hedlund twice this fall. He should absolutely be right up there battling with the other two contenders.
