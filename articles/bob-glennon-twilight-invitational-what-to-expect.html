Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational: What to Expect

A look at the top teams, individual contenders, and biggest matchups heading into the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational.

By Ethan Barossi  ·  October 8, 2026

Although there was a good amount of action in Massachusetts this week, there weren't any huge upsets or breakout performances. The real drama of the week comes from the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational this weekend. We'll break down everything you need to know about the meet, including which teams and individuals have the best chances of coming out on top.

Since there isn't a Week in Review article this week, we put together a few bullet points covering the important parts of Massachusetts distance running that you might have missed.

  • BC High's Colin Kurtz has returned to the lineup. Once he gets fully back into shape, BC High should be moving up the rankings.
  • Lexington swept the Reading squad (which was missing six of its top seven runners), with a course record by Trevor Stevens in 15:46.
  • Arlington swept Belmont (which was missing its varsity squad), with a 26-second spread between its top five runners, all of whom ran personal bests.
  • Catholic Memorial rested its top runners against BC High. We suspect they're dealing with a few injuries, particularly involving Jude Kaleba.
  • Newton North won its senior-night meet against Needham and Framingham. Congratulations to Ryan Costello, Will Kenneally, Alex Daly, Matthew Gallo, Ken Matsuoka, Jonah Penn, Trent Sturtevant, Michael Dipietro, Richard Lu, and Max Nelson.
  • Needham had a strong showing, with big performances by Connor Bobbin (16:23), Austin Conti (16:31), and Cash Lindsley (17:07).
  • Concord-Carlisle had a strong showing on senior night. Congratulations to Braeden Quayle, Sawyer Bout, Ryan Perry, Jack Thomas, Rex Riessen, Andrez Gonzales, and Elliot Goodman.
  • Wellesley had a strong performance to beat Milton on senior night. Congratulations to Daniel Goldberg, Nate Flynn, Conor Foley, Aidan Abazaj, Jonas Wigneswaran, Oliver Ziggeler, Wyatt Park, Quinn Reepmeyer, Anthony Liu, Oscar Cortis, Jack Berger, Jason Chen, Domenico Stranieri, Chase Beauvais, Ryan O’Shea, Kenneth Song, David Gerson, Seth Ford, Ethan Vecchiotti, and Jayden Song.
  • North Andover showed its dominance in the MVC, beating Lowell and Lawrence on senior night. Congratulations to Tucker LaRosa, Michael Henry, Michael Tangstrom, Ethan Gunn, Colin Bissonnette, Cole Christiansen, Luke Zilliox, Cole Gordonsmith, and Ben Holland.
  • Dover-Sherborn also celebrated senior night. Congratulations to Jackson Doyle, Cash Kleiman, Leo Comsikey, and Billy Hardy.

The main team battle up front seems to be between Brookline and Cambridge. Liam Hartmann and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem have another duo to battle up front in Thomas Dewitt and Leon Ibanez-Fraile. However, the meet will probably be decided by their depth. Although, on paper, Brookline's third through fifth runners all come in before Cambridge's fifth runner, Cambridge's third and fourth runners both come in before Brookline's third. It's hard to say which team will win at this point. On paper, the Cambridge team we left off with last November seems to be faster than this Brookline team. However, the question still stands: Is this the Cambridge team we saw last fall?

Don't count Brookline out of anything, especially at this meet. Last year, this was the meet where they proved they were capable of winning their fourth title, taking an upset victory over Natick. At this meet, Hartmann upset Natick's John Bianchi with a big PR of 15:04, while Abdel-Dayem outkicked Natick's Freddie Van Der Velden and Declan Hava down the final stretch. Could we see the Warriors pull off a similar feat?

We'd put Newton North as this meet's main sleeper team. One of the reasons for this is that they switched up their meet schedule to come to this meet. Originally, they were going to Fort Devens this weekend but switched to the Twilight meet, presumably for better competition. This is also their first invitational of the season, so we're hoping to see big drops in time from their entire squad.

We're also excited to see how some other teams perform this weekend, as we haven't really seen their true capabilities. The Longmeadow squad is running its first all-out 5K since the beginning of September, although we're pretty sure its home course was a bit short. We haven't seen the full potential of the Reading team yet, especially because they sat most of their lineup during their dual meet against Lexington this week. We're hoping they were only resting their runners so they could perform at this meet. The same goes for Natick. They're bringing back lots of talent and depth, although we don't think we've seen their full potential yet, especially in their top three runners. Belmont runs its first all-out 5K this weekend. We know they're fast due to their upset victory over Reading, but that was on a three-mile course.

On the individual side, there's definitely a lot of talent, although our three main favorites are Anthony Malakidis, Liam Hartmann, and Ryan Costello. Malakidis and Hartmann are currently the two fastest entries for the meet, having faced off last weekend at the Bay State Invitational. However, Ryan Costello has been the clear favorite in Massachusetts throughout September. Just because he didn't race last weekend doesn't mean he's slower than the rest of the competition. We're also expecting runners like Patrick Geagan, Landon Zoss, and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem to be in the mix.

In the JV race, Newton North holds six of the top seven seeds, although we're highly confident that their home course is actually short. Eli Billings (16:31) and Will Kenneally (16:35) lead the Newton North squad. However, we wouldn't sleep on Brookline. They've won the JV race for the past three years, and we doubt they'll want to give up their title without a fight. Brookline is led by Nam Danh, Noah Karp, and Wei Chandler, while the Newton North squad is rounded out by Max Yee, Lucca Biello, Ken Matsuoka, and Jonah Penn. Keep your eyes on Concord-Carlisle's Cam Smack, though. He comes in with a 17:21 season best, but a PR of 16:23.

In the sophomore race, Concord-Carlisle seems to be running away with it. They hold seven of the top 16 entries and four of the top five seeds. Will Okie and Nick Baughman lead this squad from the front.

Medium School 5K

Key Teams: Weston / Groton-Dunstable / Northampton / Amherst-Pelham

Key Individuals:

  • Andrew Kosiba – Groton-Dunstable
  • Greyson Duane – Groton-Dunstable
  • Calvin Miller – Amherst-Pelham
  • Benjamin Barron – Whitinsville Christian
  • Hunter Waechter – Weston
  • Mathew Fitzgibbon – Weston
  • Nate Palmieri – Pembroke
  • Logan Holloway – Pembroke

Don't judge this race based on the Athletic.net entries because we can assure you that you'll end up surprised. On paper, it looks like Northampton is running away with this title, although we don't think that will be anywhere near the truth. The main battle seems to be between Groton-Dunstable and Weston, with Northampton actually looking a bit farther back in third.

Of the two teams, we'd actually give Weston the edge, even with one of their top returners, Aarav Mehta, entered in the JV race. Mathew Fitzgibbon comes in at the bottom of the list due to him not having run a 5K yet this year. However, he is one of Division 2's top returners. Hunter Waechter is currently the No. 2 seed after a big PR last weekend of 15:37. Weston's Jonah Watkins is currently the No. 9 seed to back his teammates up. He's only a freshman and comes in with a PR of 16:31. Weston's Kevin Han comes in a bit farther back with a PR of 16:50. However, he comes in with a two-mile PR of 9:55, so he should theoretically be able to bring his time way down.

Don't sleep on Groton-Dunstable, though. They've got a really good group of returners; the question is just what they can do with them. Andrew Kosiba and Greyson Duane seem to be the only one-two punch that can challenge the Weston duo. Kosiba, in particular, is the No. 1 seed in this race and in all of Division 2. Micah Pierantozzi and Ashvik Yadav open their seasons this weekend. They boast PRs of 16:28 and 16:44. The Duane brothers round out the squad, with Cameron Duane entering with a PR of 16:59 and a season best of 17:00.

The individual side seems to be very tight, with five runners hunting for the title. Kosiba, Miller, Palmieri, Fitzgibbon, and Waechter all seem to be in the mix, although Kosiba is clearly the main favorite. He had a big showing last weekend, winning the Twilight B race in a time of 15:30. We wouldn't sleep on Nate Palmieri, though. The Pembroke standout has his first all-out attempt this weekend after running a 9:22 two-mile last spring at the Weston Twilight Invitational.

Small School 5K

Key Teams: Parker Charter / Hopedale

Key Individuals:

  • Quinn Cook – Hopedale
  • Ben Stone – Hopedale
  • Jett Johnson – Parker Charter
  • Konrad Schluter – Maynard
  • Matthew Pilon – Lunenburg
  • Nick Rivera – East Longmeadow
  • Zachary Mazzaferro – East Longmeadow

Oh, we're looking at the main matchup of the weekend right here. On paper, Parker Charter doesn't actually look to be the favorite, as one of their top runners isn't entered in the race. The Hopedale boys have the first shot at taking down the Division 3 champions, and we think they'll step up to the challenge. Without one of Parker Charter's top runners, Hopedale actually looks to be the favorite for the race. Can their top-end talent and tight pack be the key to finally dethroning Parker Charter?

On the individual side, Quinn Cook enters as the clear favorite. Hopedale has the chance to take both the individual and team titles in Division 3. However, Nick Rivera and Konrad Schluter both had strong performances last weekend. Schluter, in particular, was pacing most of the Twilight A race before falling off toward the end. If he doesn't set the pace this weekend, he has the opportunity to run a much faster time. Parker Charter's Jett Johnson also looks to be a top competitor. He didn't have his best race last weekend, but he's definitely the type of runner who will want to challenge Cook.

In the Small School sophomore race, Trevor Sullivan is entered as the top seed. The Division 3 star comes into this weekend with a PR of 15:20 from the Bay State Invitational. Let's see if he can bring it down even more.
