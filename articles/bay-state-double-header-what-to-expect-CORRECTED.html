Bay State Double Header: What to Expect

A look at the teams, individuals, and biggest matchups heading to Wrentham this weekend.

By Ethan Barossi
The start of the 2023 D2A race. Photo: Kenny Forester

There's a lot of action going on this week, all taking place at the Wrentham Development Center. With two different meets and many teams looking to come away with a win, we’ll break down the races, teams, individuals, and what you can expect this weekend.

MSTCA Frank Kelley Invitational:

Key Teams - Hopedale, Wellesley, Northampton, Oliver Ames, Groton-Dunstable, Brookline

Key Individuals -
Quinn Cook - Hopedale
Mathew Pilon - Lunenburg
Daniel Goldberg - Wellesley
Andrew Kosiba - Groton-Dunstable
Greyson Duane - Groton-Dunstable
Devon McStowe - Oliver Ames
Landon Zoss - Andover

Oliver Ames looks to be the clear favorite for the 5K. Although they're out one of their top returners, they bring in four runners who should all be near the front. Devon McStowe leads this squad, followed by Corey Yifru, Peter Feoderoff, and Jake Hauvuy. Their #5 runner then comes in a bit farther back, with Nicholas Colvin entering with an 18:20 seed. Although, it's important to keep in mind that it doesn’t look like this team has run an all-out 5K yet this year, so all of their times should go way down.

If any team is going to challenge Oliver Ames, we believe it will be either Hopedale or Wellesley. The Wellesley squad enters its entire team and is led by two really strong runners in Daniel Goldberg and Nate Flynn. The squad also contains lots of depth, having another six runners below the 17:30 mark.

Hopedale also comes in with its entire squad and a lot of top-end talent. Quinn Cook should hope to be at the front of the race, coming off a freshman campaign where he made the podium in the All-State two-mile. He's backed up by Ben Stone and Connor Fitzgibbon. Stone is a strong runner himself, being the #3 returner from the Division 3 Meet of Champions. Hopedale’s #4 and #5 then come in around 17:30.

It will be interesting to see where teams like Brookline and Groton-Dunstable line up. Both teams entered are definitely not at full strength. Brookline entered its entire JV lineup for this race, and Groton-Dunstable has only four of its top seven runners, with its #5 coming in with an 18:59 seed. Still, we’re excited to see where Groton-Dunstable stands in terms of its top end, especially its top two.

On the individual side, there's not necessarily a clear favorite. It should be a close race, with every single one of our key individuals hoping to be just under the 16:00 barrier. Andrew Kosiba has his first all-out attempt after an eighth-place finish in the All-State mile last spring. The same goes for Greyson Duane. He didn’t have his best junior-year campaign, but it’ll be interesting to see if he can step up. Landon Zoss is coming off a big individual win over Billerica’s Mason Niles from earlier in the week. Daniel Goldberg is the only individual who has tried to challenge Newton North’s Ryan Costello this season. Quinn Cook also had a big early-season statement, running 16:09 at the Highland Park Invitational earlier this year. It’ll be interesting to see how all of these individuals match up.

MSTCA Bay State Invitational:

This meet randomly spreads all entered teams into two different races, so we’ll be reviewing each individually.

A Race -

Key Teams - Billerica, Brookline, Concord-Carlisle, Lincoln-Sudbury, Newton South

Key Individuals -
Mason Niles - Billerica
Sahil Gandhi - Billerica
Liam Hartmann - Brookline
Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem - Brookline
Braeden Quayle - Concord-Carlisle
Anthony Malakidis - North Attleboro
Zachary Mazzaferro - East Longmeadow
Nick Rivera - East Longmeadow

After a whole month of the season, we finally get to see some of our top teams in the state run some all-out 5Ks, and we’re especially excited for this matchup.

#2 Brookline faces off against #4 Concord-Carlisle for which team will win this race. These two teams play off each other really well. Yes, Brookline has lots of strength in its back-end pack, but it particularly has the most strength in its top two runners: Liam Hartmann and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem. Concord-Carlisle, on the other hand, does have some top-end talent in Braeden Quayle, but its pack of runners is what separates the team. It’ll be interesting to see if that pack of Brookline runners can stick with the Concord-Carlisle pack. Sawyer Bout, Elijah Liang, Cameron Smack, Ryan Perry, and Toby Martel will be facing off against Matt Ritter, Nam Danh, Elias Thomasson, Noah Karp, and Toby Paradise.

However, we would still give Brookline the edge here. Again, Jackson Misek is not entered in this race, so Concord-Carlisle is still without its #1 runner. Even without him, though, we believe this team can still hold its own.

On the individual side, there is a ridiculous amount of talent. Earlier in the season, we made an article about eight different runners who we thought had a legit shot at becoming state champion. Flash forward to now, and five of those individuals are all facing off: Anthony Malakidis, Liam Hartmann, Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem, Mason Niles, and Sahil Gandhi. As of right now, it seems likely to be between three of these candidates, with Abdel-Dayem and Gandhi being a bit farther back. Still, this race is going to be very tight on both the individual and team sides.

B Race -

Key Teams - Boston Latin Academy, North Andover, Parker Charter, St. John’s Prep, Walpole, Weston, Weymouth

Key Individuals -
Andrew Kosiba - Groton-Dunstable
Brandon Spiess - Boston Latin Academy
Mathew Fitzgibbon - Weston
Aarav Mehta - Weston
Patrick Geagan - Weymouth
Jett Johnson - Parker Charter
Leo Seltenrich - St. John’s Prep
Henry McDonnell - St. John’s Prep

Even though they aren’t in the same race, #3 St. John’s Prep will still be on the same terrain as Brookline and Concord-Carlisle, so we’ll still be able to get an accurate matchup of their talent. Prep should be running away with the title in the B race after handling themselves against a talented BC High team.

Though second place should be a legitimate battle. We believe the main contenders are Boston Latin Academy and North Andover, but Weston and Parker Charter open their seasons this weekend, and they have a good chance to pull off an upset.

North Andover has the edge here. They have a tight pack of six runners with what's currently one of the lowest spreads in the state. Tucker LaRosa, Liam Rand, Tristan Rand, Owen Desmand, Cameron Riccio, and Will Thomas should be able to move up through the race together, intimidating the other teams.

Though the pack from Boston Latin Academy could either be right with North Andover or even have a chance of being in front. Brandon Spiess leads this squad, followed by three more strong runners in Adam Kramer, Khalid Jama, and Nate Iliff. Though the main problem with BLA is their #5. They have their first real opener this weekend, so we don’t particularly know if they have another solid runner, but we’re keeping them below North Andover for now because their #5 is so far back on paper.

On the individual side, it looks like Weymouth’s Patrick Geagan has the edge here. He’s one of the state’s top returners and has some crazy leg speed. Though Groton-Dunstable’s Andrew Kosiba is currently entered in both invitationals, if he decides to run at night, he looks to be Geagan’s main competition. Weston’s Mathew Fitzgibbon also seems to be one of the top competitors.

This meet should give us a great look at some of the top teams in the rankings, especially our #2-4 teams in Brookline, St. John’s Prep, and Concord-Carlisle, and how they all match up against each other. We’re specifically excited for Brookline. The four-time state champions have something to prove this year. Although they lose lots of senior talent every year, they usually have a way of finding their way back to the top.
