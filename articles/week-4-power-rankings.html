Week 4 Power Rankings

The rankings see their biggest shakeup yet after major upsets during the week and plenty of drama at Wrentham.

By Ethan Barossi · October 5, 2026

Newton South Boys at the Bay State Invitational

With a few major upsets during the week and plenty of drama at the Wrentham Development Center over the weekend, the rankings had their biggest shakeup yet. We’ll give an in-depth review of the new Top 20 as we head into the month of October.

#2 Brookline

The Warriors hold on to the #2 spot but move even farther away from the #3 and #4 ranked teams. They had a victorious showing at the Bay State Invitational, placing first overall in the A Race. Recent Boston College commit Liam Hartmann (15:23) and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem (15:35) proved their top-end strength. Elias Thomasson (16:09), Toby Paradise (16:15), and Matt Ritter (16:16) followed closely behind, with all five finishing inside the top 25.

Brookline faces off against #1 Cambridge Rindge & Latin this weekend, and we’re excited to see who comes out on top.

#3 North Andover

North Andover made the biggest jump in the rankings this week. They made a compelling statement in the Bay State B Race to show that they belong among the state’s elite.

Will Thomas, Tucker LaRosa, and Liam Rand all finished below 16:00. Owen Desmand, Tristan Rand, and Cameron Riccio all finished below 16:30. Finally, Nate Zwinggi, Luka Radivojevic, and Carter Eastwick-Haskall all finished below 16:40.

Last year, Cambridge Rindge & Latin made a similar statement at this meet, going from unranked to #4. This year, North Andover proved why they were one of the biggest sleepers in the state.

#4 St. John’s Prep

St. John’s Prep takes the loss to North Andover at the Bay State Invitational, although the results may be a bit deceiving.

Leo Seltenrich, Mateo DeOrio, and Liam Callahan all finished below 16:00, with Sam Valentine coming in at 16:34. However, Henry McDonnell was in the JV race, where he won with a time of 16:38, although his PR is well below 16:00.

Prep and North Andover should go back and forth over these next few weeks, as both teams are very similar in skill.

#6 Concord-Carlisle

Concord-Carlisle moves down below Newton North this week after their pack finished a bit farther back than we thought.

They were led by Braeden Quayle (16:04) and Toby Martel (16:10). Then Elijah Liang ran 16:24, Sawyer Bout ran 16:25, Will Okie ran 16:29, Ryan Perry ran 16:31, Nick Baughman ran 16:32, and Jack Thomas ran 16:32.

We pictured this pack a bit closer to the 16:20 range, so we think the Newton North pack might have the edge. Still, we’re excited to see how both of these teams perform at Twilight this weekend.

#7 Parker Charter

Division 3 went crazy this weekend, with one of the biggest performances coming from Parker Charter.

They go all the way from #17 to #7.

Jett Johnson (15:50) leads this team, followed by a pack of three more runners below 16:20. Nathan Davis anchors the squad at 16:54. We were definitely sleeping on this squad, and they showed that even with their small school size, they’re going to be a tough team to beat.

#10 Reading Memorial

This is one of the more confusing teams on the list. We know they’re one of the state’s top teams, but their performance against Belmont didn’t make them look as strong. Still, we’re keeping them at #10.

We know they’re currently without one of their top runners in Andrew Princic, but Max King still seems to be healthy and ready to go. We’re also hoping last week’s performance was just an off day for Finn Johnson-Houlihan.

Still, they had two runners commit to Division I colleges in the past week, so we’re going to trust these runners’ abilities.

Congratulations to Finn Johnson-Houlihan, who will be running for Bryant, and Max King, who will be running for Merrimack College.

#11 Lincoln-Sudbury & #13 Newton South

Both of these teams go from unranked to the middle of the pack after great performances at the Bay State Invitational.

Lincoln-Sudbury has the advantage here because of their top-end strength. They have three runners below 16:00, with 16:49 and 17:16 rounding out the squad. Although those final two runners are a bit farther back, we have Lincoln-Sudbury ahead of Newton South because of that heavy top-end.

Newton South enters slightly behind with a team that looks a lot different. This team’s strength is its pack. They have four runners between 16:09 and 16:22. That’s only 13 seconds separating their #1 through #4. Alan Herring rounds out the squad at 16:59.

Just because we grouped these teams together doesn’t mean we’re undermining their performances. Both teams just made huge moves to climb up the rankings and race each other multiple times throughout the season. We’re excited to see which of these two teams comes out on top.

#14 Weston

Weston enters as the No. 1 seed for Division 2 after showing off its depth at the Bay State Invitational.

Hunter Waechter placed fourth overall in the B Race, running a huge PR of 15:37. It seems as though Mathew Fitzgibbon was only resting this week, as he’s entered for the Twilight Invitational this weekend. When both runners are racing, they’ll form a nasty 1-2 punch.

Jonah Watkins (16:31), Kevin Han (16:50), and Thomas Moser (17:09) round out the squad.

The cherry on top is Aarav Mehta (15:55), although we don’t know how healthy he is. We believe Mehta is currently on his way back from injury, and if he does come back, Weston moves even higher in the rankings with an amazing 1-2-3 punch, especially for Division 2.

Though, it’s important to note that Mehta is not currently entered for Twilight.

#15 Hopedale

After a whole month of waiting, the Hopedale boys finally find themselves entering the Top 20.

Quinn Cook leads the squad up front with a time of 15:28. If Cook races at Twilight, this time could definitely go even lower.

They then have a tight pack of four more runners averaging around 16:40 for 5K. Ben Stone, Connor Fitzgibbon, Cedric Author, and Jack Murphy round out the squad.

Keep an eye out for Hopedale, as they have their eyes set on taking Parker Charter’s crown.

#18 Billerica Memorial

This team was also really tricky to rank. Billerica in a dual meet and Billerica in an invitational can look like two completely different teams. We decided on #18 because it feels like the middle ground between both scoring styles.

Mason Niles and Sahil Gandhi lead this squad from the front. We knew this duo would be fast, but they’re currently two of the fastest individuals in the state. Going 1-2 in any race spells trouble for the opposing team.

Max Gorski (16:51) and Dylan Kane (16:57) are the squad’s #3 and #4. Although their #5 comes in at 17:39, Billerica usually has another runner up with Kane during its dual meets who didn’t race this weekend: Shaunak Kulkarni.

With two runners up front and potentially three more below 17:00, this team moves up to #18.

#19 Groton-Dunstable

These final two teams confuse us the most because we don’t really know their entire lineups.

Andrew Kosiba is now the definite individual to beat in Division 2. He ran a blistering 15:30 on Saturday night to move himself even higher among the state’s top individuals.

We expected Greyson Duane to be a bit closer to him. However, a 16:20 opener is still very impressive, especially considering he ran in the morning while Kosiba raced at night.

After their top two, though, we don’t really know what their full team looks like. Cameron Duane ran 17:00 Saturday morning, with many of their top returners not racing at all.

Still, we believe they’ll have something to prove with their full team entered in this weekend’s Twilight Invitational.

#20 BC High

Without Colin Kurtz, BC High moves even further down to #20. They lost their matchup with Xaverian, although if Kurtz had raced, they most likely would have won.

Still, Joseph Hayes, Theo Weiss, and Nolan Kurtz provide enough talent to keep this team in the Top 20.

All these moves for BC High are really just us trying to figure out their lineup. We had them at #3 when we thought they had Owen Geagan. Then they moved to #7 when we suspected he was out. Now they’re #20 because we believe Kurtz is out as well.

Just remember: if BC High has its full lineup, they can still challenge some of the best teams in the state.
