Through the Storm: Weekend in Review

Hopedale makes a statement, Weston wins a tight Division 2 battle, and top teams deliver across Massachusetts.

By Ethan Barossi  •  October 12, 2026
Hopedale’s Quinn Cook on his way to a win and MA #2 time of 15:15 at the Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational. Photo: Bryan Stone, @stonephotolens. Photo: @stonephotolens

We won’t gloss over what happened Sunday, but there’s still a lot to celebrate from this weekend. We want to say that we truly do appreciate every person and athlete who showed up on Sunday night, regardless of if you ran or not. The majority of people came there to race for around 20 minutes or just to watch a single race, yet were still trying to put up with the weather conditions for hours on end. Many athletes came in hoping or even expecting a PR, but we want to make it clear that the course was not in PR shape on Sunday night. Again, thank you to every person who showed up and truly cares about this sport and community.

On the brighter side, the small school championship ended up having some big standouts along with some key performances from the Wickham Park Invitational, Devens Championship Classic, and Manhattan University Invitational. This article will break down everything you missed this weekend in Massachusetts cross country.

Bob Glennon Twilight Invitational (Small Schools)

**Small School Sophomore Race - **

From the gun to the finish line, Trevor Sullivan took control. The Granby star ran a blistering 15:35 and did it all by himself. Sullivan took the lead immediately and had already gapped the field by the first lap of the course.

Watch out for this star. Like a few others, he’s got his eyes on the Division 3 title and can clearly hold his own in a race.

**Small School Varsity Race - **

PRs and great performances across the board. Quinn Cook won the overall race in an amazing time of 15:15. The Hopedale star started the race in second behind Maynard’s Konrad Schluter (15:29), but then fully took over the race after the first kilometer. This performance puts Cook at MA #2, beating out Billerica's **Mason Niles’s **performance from last weekend.

Frontier’s **Evan Hudland **(15:24) finished the race in second overall. It appears he had a more conservative approach to this race which may have backfired. He didn’t go out with Cook and Schluter on the first lap, and although this allowed him to move up throughout the race, Cook had created too big of a gap for Hudland to close.

Schluter finished in third overall, being outkicked by Hudland in the last lap. Parker Charter’s Jett Johnson finished 4th overall in 15:39. Then it was actually the other member of the East Longmeadow duo who had the breakout performance. Last weekend Nick Rivera **crossed the line in 15:41, gapping his teammate. This week **Zachary Mazzaferro pulled off the better performance, placing 5th with a time of 15:39. Lunenburg’s Mathew Pilon then rounded out the top group with a 6th-place finish in 15:42.

As we head into November, all of these individuals should be in contention for the Division 3 individual title. Schluter, Johnson, Mazzaferro, Rivera, and Pilon will all be in the pack, though the current favorites are Cook, Sullivan, and Hudland.

On the team side, Hopedale had a day. It was clear that Parker Charter didn’t have their full lineup, however, the Hopedale boys completely surpassed expectations. After** Quinn Cook** won the race, two of his teammates had some big PRs to both place inside the top 12. Ben Stone **placed 10th overall with an 11-second PR and 35 seconds faster than his SB from last weekend. **Connor Fitzgibbon followed by an even more impressive performance. He placed 12th overall with a big 30-second PR.

Cedric Author **(17:00) and **Jack Murphy **(17:09) rounded out Hopedale’s top 5. Although these runners didn’t have their best day, the team's depth was on full display. **Ethan DeWolf, Hopedale’s #6 runner, followed right behind Murphy to run 17:10. DeWolf had a big 37-second PR, and connected himself up with members of the Hopedale pack.

Hopedale won the meet with 94 points, while Mt. Greylock placed 2nd with 156. Reigning Division 3 champions Parker Charter placed 6th with 228. This performance moves Hopedale to the #1 seed for Division 3 and shows that this might be the year Parker Charter’s streak ends.

Medium School Varsity Race -

On the individual side, it was a battle. Many individuals took the lead and made big moves, though the smartest race of the night may have belonged to Weston’s Hunter Waechter. Waechter won the race overall in a PR of 15:29. This currently makes Waechter the #1 seed for Division 2 after beating out a really stacked field.

Whitinsville Christian’s Benjamin Barron placed 2nd with a big breakout performance. We didn’t even have Barron in our runners to watch, though he had a big 25-second PR and ran a very smart race to come out in second with a time of 15:34.

Amherst-Pelham’s Calvin Miller placed 3rd overall in 15:39. Groton-Dunstable’s Andrew Kosiba **placed 4th in 15:46. Based on his performance last weekend it appears that Kosiba did not have his best day. We didn’t have Ashland’s **Luca Domestico in our runners to watch either, though he proved himself by placing 5th overall in 15:48. There were some other key runners who fell off the pack a bit but we’re assuming that they just weren’t on their game on Saturday night. Keep your eyes on Pembroke’s Nate Palmieri, Weston’s Mathew Ftizgibbon, and Amherst-Pelham’s Peter Nedeau, as they’re all key runners in Division 2.

On the team side, although the race wasn’t as close as last year, it was still very competitive. Weston took the title with 88 points, Amherst-Pelham surpassed expectations to place 2nd with 101 points, Northampton finished 3rd with 111, and Groton-Dunstable finished 4th with 121 points.

Weston’s victory is even more impressive when you consider that this squad was nowhere near full strength. Aarav Mehta, their #2 returner, tempo’d the JV race as he's coming back from injury. Beware of Mehta when he fully comes back into play as he boasts a PR of 15:55. After Waechter won the race, Mathew Fitzgibbon followed behind to place 12th. We don’t think Fitzgibbon is at full strength either. He sat out last weekend’s twilight meet and is also one of Division 2’s top returners.

Freshman Jonah Watkins placed 21st overall with a PR of 16:25. Thomas Moser followed closely behind with a breakout performance. Moser placed 27th overall with a time of 16:33, 36 seconds faster than his previous personal best. **Kevin Han **rounded out the squad in 16:56.

Amherst-Pelham surpassed our expectations to place 2nd overall. After hearing that they were out one of their top returners we moved this squad down in the rankings; however, this proves they’re still a force to be reckoned with in Division 2. We were particularly invested in the team's depth: Nicholas Workman ran 16:48 for a big 68-second PR, Alhassan Abdel-Maksoud **rounded out the squad in 17:15 for a 36 second PR. **Otis Fairey also played a role placing 15th overall in a time of 16:06, a 32-second PR for the runner.

After their loss last weekend, the Northampton squad had a big day. Their entire top four runners had big PRs, leading the team to a third place finish. This team entered the season a bit under our radar, but they are now one of the top contending teams in Division 2.

They have a big trio up front in** Shea O’Neil** (16:10), Henry Daggett (16:17), and Charlie Sidoti **(16:28). **Owen Daggett (16:48) and** Gus Frey** (16:50) then round out this squad. Although Frey was the only one of their top runners to not PR yesterday, he comes into the season with a 16:39 PR.

However, this performance from Groton-Dunstable doesn’t tell the whole story. Their #2 runner, Greyson Duane, was actually missing from the race. If Groton had Duane in its lineup, it most likely would’ve walked away with the title. Their performance last night is also highlighted by big PRs from Micah Pierantozzi (16:02), and** Ashton Duane** (16:26).

So the main Division 2 battle still seems to be between Weston and Groton. The question is which of these teams will reach its full potential.

43rd Wickham Park XC Invitational

Lexington traveled to the harsh terrain of Wickham Park on Saturday and ran away with the win. Patrick Noonan **(15:50) won the race overall in his biggest performance of the year. **Trevor Stevens (15:57) followed closely behind in second. These times are even more impressive when you consider their dual meet with Reading earlier in the week. Noonan actually ran faster at Wickham, while Stevens ran only 11 seconds slower. Keep in mind that Wickham is substantially harder. Alden Hall (16:39), Benjamin Sbihli (17:07), and **Matteo Sanchez De Rojas **(17:10) rounded out this squad.

Lexington entered the preseason as one of the state's top favorites but has fallen down a few spots over the last couple months. This performance shows why we were so high on this team. Even after losing multiple of their top runners this team still seems to have something to prove.

Devens Pre-Meet of Champions

With many teams deciding to take part in Twilight this year, only one major contender went up to Devens this weekend. North Andover absolutely destroyed the competition placing six runners in the top 12 and nine in the top 19. Liam Rand was the squad’s top finisher, running 16:32 for 2nd. Luka Radivojevic (16:37) and Tristand Rand (16:40) then crossed the line together to finish 5th and 6th overall. Tucker LaRosa (16:52) finished 8th, then Cameron Riccio **(17:03) and **Michael LeGros **(17:08) finished 11th and 12th. LeGros was actually on their JV team last weekend at the Bay State Invitational, though on Saturday he was their sixth-best runner and the only one on the team to PR. It’s also important to note that this team was clearly not at full strength. Their #1 runner, **Will Thomas, and #4, Owen Desmond, both did not compete on Saturday.

Manhattan University Invitational

The Xaverian and Catholic Memorial squads both headed over to New York for this meet. Although it was clear that neither squad had its best day, there were some key individual performances at the meet. Congratulations to Catholic Memorial’s Gerard Stock (13:27) on his 12th-place finish in the “F” race, and Xaverian’s Jack Weintraub (12:51) on his exciting win in the “E” race.
