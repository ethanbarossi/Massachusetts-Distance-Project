2026–27 Bay State Conference Indoor Track Preview

By Ethan Barossi

The cross-country season has officially come to an end, and it's time to turn our attention to indoor track. The Bay State Conference is home to some of the most talented athletes in Massachusetts, and this winter should be no different. With some incredibly well-rounded teams, talented returning athletes, and individuals who made significant improvements during the outdoor season, there will be plenty to keep an eye on.

For our first indoor track preview of the season, we'll be breaking down both the boys' and girls' sides of the Bay State Conference, looking at team rankings, individual talent, and the biggest strengths of each team heading into the winter.

So let's dive right in.

#1 Newton North

Newton North enters the indoor season as our favorite to take home the BSC title. What makes this team so dangerous is their ability to pick up points across nearly every event.

The Tigers return an incredibly talented sprint group featuring Oliver Smith and William Balerna, both coming off impressive outdoor seasons.

Perhaps their biggest advantage over the rest of the conference comes in the field events. Theo Ezzat jumped 22'4.25" during the outdoor season, while Julius Murinzi improved his shot put personal best from 43'7" to 50'2".

On the distance side, Ryan Costello is coming off an outdoor season where he ran 4:21.70 in the mile and 9:12.31 in the two-mile. Alongside him, Jamie Evans should be a major threat in the middle-distance events.

There really aren't many weaknesses on this team, and it'll take an incredible performance from another program to take them down.

#2 Brookline

Coming off their fifth consecutive cross-country All-State title, it should come as no surprise that Brookline will have an incredibly talented distance group this winter.

One athlete we are particularly excited to see return to the indoor track is Liam Hartmann. After dealing with an injury last winter, Hartmann returns with PRs of 4:24 in the mile and 9:12 in the two-mile.

Alongside him, Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem, Matthew Ritter, Elias Thomasson, and Toby Paradise give Brookline an incredible amount of depth.

Outside of the distance events, Will Southmayd returns with a 6.91 personal best in the 55m, while Daniel Rosen could be an important contributor in both the hurdles and high jump. Alex Golosovker is another athlete to watch after improving his long jump personal best to 19'10.5" outdoors.

Brookline should be able to pick up a significant number of points in the distance events, but their ability to compete with Newton North in the shorter events and field events could ultimately determine whether they take home the conference title.

#3 Needham

Needham arguably has some of the most exciting individual athletes in the entire conference.

Elias Langsner returns with a 6.62 personal best in the 55m and is coming off an outdoor season where he ran an incredible 21.94 in the 200m.

Another athlete we're incredibly excited to watch is Nathan Cunoosamy. After running 1:33.43 in the 600m last winter, Cunoosamy went on to run 49.48 in the 400m outdoors. He also returns with a 6'0" high jump personal best, making him an incredibly valuable athlete for this team.

Gavin Prout will be another important contributor in the field events, while Connor Bobbin, Duncan Alexander, and Austin Conti are coming off impressive cross-country seasons.

With the amount of talent Needham has across the different event groups, the Rockets could be in for a special season.

#4 Milton

Although Milton enters the season at #4, they certainly have the individual talent to compete with the three teams ahead of them.

Jonah Stephens Roye returns as one of the conference's top hurdlers with a personal best of 7.77, while Noah Tran will be another important contributor in the sprints.

Milton also returns some incredible talent in the jumping events. Taj Chung jumped 22'3.5" outdoors, while Teague McWeeny returns with a 6'0" high jump personal best.

One of the most talented returning middle-distance runners in the conference is Robert Beato, who ran an impressive 2:31.92 in the 1000m last winter.

Milton's strength in the field events could make a significant difference in some of their tougher dual meets this season.

#5 Walpole

Walpole enters the season with a number of athletes capable of making an impact across multiple events.

Benjamin Thomas is one of the most versatile athletes on this team, coming off an outdoor season where he ran 51.45 in the 400m while also being a strong contributor in the jumping events.

Kyle Veilleux will be someone to watch in the shot put after throwing 45'6.5" outdoors, while Anson Hung will be an important contributor in the hurdles.

On the distance side, Nate O'Neil and Owen Ahlfont have put down some very impressive times.

Walpole has the potential to be a very competitive dual-meet team.

#6 Framingham

Framingham has some serious individual talent, particularly in the sprints and jumping events.

Blessed Tenthani returns with a 6.68 personal best in the 55m and ran 22.05 in the 200m outdoors.

Alongside him, Mason Saporetti Berberian returns with a 35.97 personal best in the 300m and an outdoor long jump personal best of 22'9". Having two athletes of this caliber could make Framingham incredibly difficult to beat in the shorter events.

Luke Miller returns with an 8.07 personal best in the 55m hurdles, while Andrew Terranova will be an important contributor in the shot put.

Framingham has some of the best individual talent in the conference, and if they can develop more depth across the other events, they could certainly move inside our top 5.

#7 Natick

One of the biggest names to watch on this Natick team is Callum Doyle, who is coming off an incredible cross-country season and returns with a 1:57.71 personal best in the 800m.

Declan Hava and Alex Forte will also be important contributors throughout the distance events.

In the sprints, Aidan Cliver will be an athlete to keep an eye on, while Brandon Leshinski is coming off an outdoor season where he ran 11.80 in the 100m.

Natick has some talented individuals, but it'll be interesting to see how much depth they can develop outside of the distance events.

#8 Braintree

Braintree returns one of the conference's top throwers in Robert Nuvoli, who threw an impressive 50'0.5" last indoor season.

Adrian Lugo and Lovelle Simmons will also be important athletes for the Wamps this winter.

One of the biggest improvements on the distance side came from Kyle Koca, who went from running 4:59.80 in the mile indoors to 4:38.74 outdoors.

Braintree has several athletes capable of winning individual events, and their ability to pick up points behind those athletes will be important throughout the season.

#9 Wellesley

Wellesley returns some exciting talent in both the sprints and field events.

Edward Billy returns with a 6.82 personal best in the 55m, while Chase Martin is coming off an outdoor season where he jumped 20'5.5" in the long jump.

In the distance events, Daniel Goldberg ran 10:03.61 in the two-mile outdoors, while Oliver Zinggeler will be another athlete to keep an eye on.

Although Wellesley enters the season at #9, there are several areas where this team could surprise us.

#10 Weymouth

Despite entering the season at #10, Weymouth returns one of the most talented distance runners in the entire conference in Patrick Geagan.

Geagan returns with personal bests of 2:36.35 in the 1000m and 9:28.30 in the two-mile and is coming off an impressive cross-country season.

Andrew Himmer and Max Robertson will also be athletes to watch, while Ray Khalil should be an important contributor in the shot put after throwing 38'8" outdoors.

It'll be interesting to see which younger athletes step up for the Wildcats this winter.

#1 Newton North

Newton North enters the indoor season as our favorite to take home both the boys' and girls' Bay State Conference titles.

One of the most exciting athletes in the entire conference is Blake MacNeal, who returns with an 8.72 personal best in the 55m hurdles and jumped an incredible 19'3.75" during the outdoor season.

Sophie Finkelstein and Skye Larson will be important contributors in the sprints, while Maggie Abely returns with a 3:01.65 personal best in the 1000m.

In the longer distance events, Esther Dudnik is coming off an outdoor season where she broke the 11-minute barrier in the two-mile, running 10:57.94.

Newton North's talent across nearly every event gives them a slight edge over Milton heading into the winter.

#2 Milton

There really isn't much separating Milton and Newton North heading into the indoor season.

Annaliese Aguilar returns with personal bests of 7.46 in the 55m and 8.43 in the 55m hurdles, while Danaya Sweeney should be a major contributor in both the sprints and long jump.

One of Milton's biggest advantages comes in the high jump, where Ella Turner returns with an incredible personal best of 5'6".

In the distance events, Grace Danielson returns with a 3:02.54 personal best in the 1000m, while Felicity Goode ran 5:14.16 in the mile outdoors.

Although Milton enters the season at #2, they have more than enough talent to take home the conference title. Their matchup against Newton North could very well be the most exciting dual meet of the entire season.

#3 Braintree

Braintree is a team we are particularly excited to watch this winter.

Chloe Dinh is coming off an incredible outdoor season, running 58.60 in the 400m and 2:13.94 in the 800m. It'll be interesting to see where she focuses her attention this winter.

Maeve McGowan and Michaela Ranieri will be important contributors in the shorter events, while Ellierose Regan gives Braintree some valuable versatility in both the hurdles and high jump.

Alongside them, Anna Flaherty returns with a 5:09.16 personal best in the mile.

Although much of the attention will be on Newton North and Milton, Braintree has the talent to make the conference title race incredibly interesting.

#4 Needham

Needham returns some incredible individual talent heading into the winter.

In the sprints, Eva Geddes returns with a 7.27 personal best in the 55m, while Sara MacConnell is coming off an outdoor season where she ran 26.40 in the 200m.

Sewanu Dansu will be an important contributor in the shot put after reaching the 30-foot barrier outdoors.

Needham also arguably has one of the most talented distance duos in the conference. Madison Roach ran an incredible 2:11.35 in the 800m outdoors, while Claire Cianciolo broke the 5-minute barrier in the mile, running 4:59.28.

Needham has several athletes capable of competing at the state level, and how they utilize those athletes throughout the dual-meet season will be interesting to watch.

#5 Wellesley

Wellesley returns some incredibly talented athletes across both the track and field events.

Leila Eccher is coming off an outdoor season where she jumped 18'9.75" in the long jump, making her one of the top athletes to watch in the conference.

Sienna Lief returns with a 7.48 personal best in the 55m, while Maisie Crissman and Madeline Tuxbury should be important contributors throughout the distance events.

Wellesley has several athletes capable of winning individual events, which could make them incredibly competitive in some of the closer dual meets this season.

#6 Weymouth

Weymouth has some exciting returning talent heading into the indoor season.

Abigail Fulton is coming off an outdoor season where she ran 27.03 in the 200m, while Georgianna Howard and Tia Khalil will also be athletes to keep an eye on.

On the distance side, Sonia Souto returns with an impressive 11:16.05 personal best in the two-mile.

Weymouth finds themselves right in the middle of the conference heading into the winter, and a few breakout performances could make a significant difference in their dual-meet season.

#7 Natick

Natick returns some valuable talent in the field events.

Amara St Cyere returns with a 29'0.75" personal best in the shot put, while Khalesi Saint improved her long jump personal best to 15'5" during the outdoor season. Grace Hamel will also be an athlete to watch in the hurdles.

In the distance events, Megan Joyce returns with a 3:05.62 personal best in the 1000m, while Margaret Armstrong gives Natick some valuable versatility between the distance events and high jump.

Natick has some interesting pieces heading into the season, and it'll be exciting to see how this team develops.

#8 Framingham

One of the athletes we're most excited to watch on this Framingham team is Kiley Lombardo, who ran an impressive 59.54 in the 400m outdoors and could be a major threat in the 600m this winter.

Bailey Goldfarb returns with a 4'8" high jump personal best, while Maya Svehlik will be an important contributor in the shot put.

Alongside them, Molly Donoghue will be another athlete to watch as Framingham looks to outperform their preseason ranking.

#9 Brookline

Leena Bhandari-Cordoba will be one of Brookline's most important athletes this winter. Returning with a 16'6.75" indoor long jump personal best and a 1:01.04 outdoor 400m, she has the ability to pick up points across multiple events.

Madelyn Carpenter, Izzy Barkoudah, and Dia Vazirani will also be important contributors for the Warriors.

Brookline has some talented individuals, and the development of their younger athletes could make a significant difference throughout the season.

#10 Walpole

Walpole returns one of the conference's top throwers in Shane Humphreys, who threw an impressive 34'6" during the outdoor season.

Jessica Borto and Leah Alves will also be athletes to keep an eye on throughout the winter.

On the distance side, Sydney Dias returns with an 11:27.78 personal best in the two-mile.
