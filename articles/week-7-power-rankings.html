Week 7 Power Rankings

Brookline keeps rolling, Lexington gets healthy, and Longmeadow continues to climb as the postseason approaches.

By Ethan Barossi · October 25, 2026

#2 Brookline

Brookline solidified itself as the #2 team in the state this week after making its dual meet against #7 Newton North look easy. The Warriors continue to separate themselves from the teams below them while closing the gap on #1 Cambridge Rindge & Latin.

Liam Hartmann (14:58) and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem (15:01) went 2–3 in what appeared to be a very conservative effort. It looked like they were trying to pull the rest of their pack along, particularly Elias Thomasson, who finished fifth overall in 15:11. Thomasson has had a great season, establishing himself as Brookline’s #3 runner and playing a key role in their wins over Newton North and at the Bay State Invitational. Matt Ritter (15:16) and Toby Paradise (15:37) rounded out the scoring five in sixth and seventh.

Hartmann and Abdel-Dayem have one more chance to go all out before the postseason, where they’ll match up again with Ryan Costello, along with other top individuals like Patrick Geagan and Declan Hava. It’ll be interesting to see what this duo can accomplish.

#6 Lexington

Lexington moves up two spots this week and looks like a much more prepared team heading into the postseason compared to where it started in September.

Going into the Middlesex League championship, the Minutemen finally appear to have their full lineup back. This is highlighted by the return of Rhys Behlings, who made his season debut last week. Behlings placed sixth overall against Belmont, only finishing behind teammates Patrick Noonan and Trevor Stevens, along with the Belmont trio. With a two-mile PR of 9:45, he should be a key part of Lexington’s lineup going forward.

Another notable entry for Monday’s varsity race is Andrew Zhang. We haven’t seen him race yet this season, but he boasts a two-mile PR of 9:57 and could provide another big boost.

With Behlings and Zhang returning alongside Patrick Noonan, Trevor Stevens, and Alden Hall, Lexington is looking like a very formidable squad.

We’re also hearing rumors that Woburn Country Club will be a full 5K this year, so it’ll be interesting to finally see what this team can run over the full distance.

#13 Longmeadow

Longmeadow is on the rise and doesn’t look to be slowing down.

The Lancers had another great performance against Northampton, with only one Northampton runner finishing ahead of Longmeadow’s fifth.

The biggest takeaway was the improvement from their back-end scorers. CJ Deliso and Jacob Abebe went 1–2 in what appeared to be a conservative effort, seemingly trying to bring teammate Finn O’Connor along. O’Connor finished right behind them in third, running 16:14.

He was followed by Aidan Kretschmar (16:25) and Henry Cournoyer (16:30). Kretschmar in particular had a great race, improving his season best by 30 seconds.

This moves Longmeadow into a similar position as Weston and makes them a real Division 2 contender. It also makes the postseason much more interesting, with some of the top Division 2 teams spread across 2A, 2B, and 2C. We could see several of these teams battling for the All-State title without even facing each other at divisionals.

#14 Reading Memorial

Reading stays at #14 this week, but we did move the Rockets ahead of Natick.

With Max King back in the lineup and MDP’s Runner of the Week Declan Grant picking up his first career victory against Winchester, Reading is starting to look much stronger.

It’s been an inconsistent season for the Rockets, but with some of their top runners returning and Grant continuing to perform, we believe this team deserves a little more credit.

Honorable Mentions

We’re changing how honorable mentions work starting this week. Previously, this section was mainly reserved for teams ranked #21–25, but we wanted to give more recognition to teams that put together strong performances throughout the week.

These aren’t necessarily the next teams in line for the rankings. Instead, they’re teams that deserve some extra credit for what they accomplished.

Needham and Wellesley

Both teams had great performances this week when they faced off in a very close dual meet.

Needham was led by Connor Bobbin, who took the individual win in 16:14. Two more Needham seniors also broke the 17-minute barrier for the first time. Cash Lindsley ran 16:52, while Duncan Alexander ran 16:59 for a huge 42-second PR. Freshman Tyler Mann also had a great day, rounding out the squad in a PR of 17:10.

However, Wellesley’s depth ultimately made the difference. Nate Flynn (16:16) put up a great battle against Bobbin, while teammate Daniel Goldberg (16:30) finished third overall.

Solomon Jin (17:06) also returned to the varsity lineup this week. It appears Jin is coming back from injury and should be a key part of the Raiders heading into the postseason.

Wellesley took the narrow 27–29 win, but both teams showed a lot of promise.

Belmont

Belmont put up a great fight against #6 Lexington this week, proving that they’re one of the stronger teams in the Middlesex League.

Their biggest strength is their trio of Will Olmstead (15:42), Noah Geiger (15:53), and Ishaan Kanagaraj (15:58). Olmstead in particular had an excellent race and just missed out on a Runner of the Week nomination.

Elias Campisano (16:59) and Paul Son (17:08) rounded out the scoring five.

Milton

Milton had a great showing against Braintree this week, putting three runners ahead of Braintree’s Kyle Koca.

Bobby Beato (15:27) led the way, followed by Gideon Toth (16:03) and Nate Turner (16:07). Toth in particular had the performance of the day, running a huge 36-second PR.

Milton has quietly been putting together a strong season and is definitely a team to keep an eye on heading into the postseason.

Lowell

Lowell took a big win over Billerica this week to finish its dual-meet season 9–1 in the MVC.

Their performance was highlighted by Samuel De Souza, who took first overall in 14:12. We don’t talk about De Souza a lot, but he’s one heck of a runner. He boasts PRs of 1:56 in the 800 meters and 4:24 in the mile.

Elijah Calderon, Brady Meade, and Maylonzo Wilson rounded out the squad with strong performances to help secure the win.

Lowell has been one of the more overlooked teams in the conference, but a 9–1 record is definitely something worth recognizing.
