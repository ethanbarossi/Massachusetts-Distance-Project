Week in Review: October 19–23

Major dual-meet battles, returning runners, and postseason contenders across Massachusetts.

By Ethan Barossi  ·  October 22, 2026

#2 Brookline Dominates #6 Newton North (23-34)

Brookline responded. After Cambridge put on a dominant performance at the Catholic Memorial Invitational, Brookline answered with a big win over conference rival Newton North.

Ryan Costellotook the individual win in 14:44 on the 2.76 mile course. The pace might look a bit off compared to what Costello normally runs but keep in mind that Larz Anderson Park is one of the most challenging courses in the state. If you need another reference to that time, Natick’s John Bianchiran 14:43 on this course his junior year.

The Brookline duo then went 2-3 in the race. We’ve seen videos of them staying with their third through fifth runners so we wouldn’t be surprised if these two specifically weren’t going all out. Liam Hartmann ran 14:58 and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem followed closely behind in 15:01.

Newton North’s Alex Dalycame in next at 15:06. This is a standout performance by Daly beating out the Brookline pack on their own turf. Keep in mind that he was also only 22 seconds behind Costello.

Though after that Brookline’s pack came into play. Junior Elias Thomasson was next to cross the line in 15:11. Thomasson has consistently proven himself as Brookline’s #3 runner all season long, expect him to play a big role going forward. Teammate Matt Ritterwas right behind in 15:16.

Brookline’s Toby Paradise then closed out the meet running 15:37 for 7th place overall. It appears that he did not have his best day being around 20 seconds behind Thomasson and Ritter.

Two more Tigers then crossed the line in Will Kenneally (15:42) and Colin Van Vliet (15:43). Kenneally specifically is a bit of an interesting case. He was their returning #3 runner on paper but has taken a bit of time to get into shape. Though he has been playing a much bigger role more recently as proven by this performance and at the Catholic Memorial Invitational.

Three more runners then crossed the line before Newton North’s #5. Brookline’s depth is highlighted by Nam Danh(15:44), Wei Chandler(15:46), and Axel Bonnafoux(15:50). Mathew Gallo then rounded out the Newton North scorers in (15:51).

This is a big win for Brookline and shows they're moving in the right direction. Based on this performance, we wouldn’t call Cambridge unbeatable by any means. This team has done it before and we believe it's possible for them to do it again.

#8 Lexington beats Belmont (24-35)

Lexington is looking to finally be back at full strength after a dominant performance over one of the stronger teams in the Middlesex League.

Patrick Noonan (15:12) and Trevor Stevens (15:16) keep proving why they are one of the top duos in the state. On the challenging 3-mile course of Rock Meadow Conservation, they had a big 1-2 finish to lead their team. Noonan also snagged the course record in the process.

The Belmont trio then came in next with another big performance fromWill Olmstead (15:42). A few weeks ago he surpassed expectations to stick with his teammates during a race to upset Reading, this week, he beat both those teammates by a considerable margin. Noah Geiger (15:53) and Ishaan Kanagaraj (15:58) followed to place 4th and 5th overall.

Another big surprise was seeing Rhys Behlings back in the lineup. This is the first time we’ve seen Behlings all season and he’s showing that he’s still in good shape taking 6th overall in 16:11. Coming into the season with a 9:45 two-mile and 16:15 5K, Behlings should be a key performer for Lexington going forward.

After the Belmont trio, Lexington took the next 7 spots. This pack was led by Behlings, Matteo Sanches De Rojas (16:15), and Ares Beaver (16:19).

#14 Reading is Getting Strong

Course note: Winchester’s course is around 100 meters short.

Reading swept Winchester with a score of 15-41 to cap off their dual meet season.

Declan Grant (16:18) led the squad, followed by Max King (16:20), Colin Herlihy (16:23), Curran Healy(16:29), andColin Carroll (16:30).

This is a great performance by Reading and shows they're moving back in the right direction. This is mainly shown through Max King’s Performance. King had DNF’d the past few races so seeing him come out and return as one of the team's top runners is very promising.

Though we do still have some questions with some of their missing members. It seems Andrew Princic may not be joining his team for the rest of the season and we still don’t know aboutFinn Johnson-Houlihan.

Whatever is going on with the Reading squad, they have 3 weeks to figure it out before the D1C championship. There we will truly see if they put it all together.

Wellesley Edges out Needham (27-29)

There was a very tight battle going on at Cutler Park Wednesday between rival teams Needham and Wellesley. Although the Raiders took the win, both teams had some great performances.

Needham’s Connor Bobbin(16:14) took the overall win here. This is a great win for the Rocket as he beat out two strong Wellesley runners in Nate Flynn (16:16) and Daniel Golderberg(16:30). Goldberg in particular has been known to run a lot faster, so we're assuming he just didn’t have his best day.

Needham’s Austin Contifollowed behind in 16:41 while Wellesley'ssConnor Foley came in at 16:45 for an 11-second PR.

After that, two more Rockets crossed the line. Cash Lindsleycame in at 16:52 for a big 15-second PR. whileDuncan Alexanderhad a day running 16:59 for a 42-second PR. Needham’s #5 runner came in a lot later but freshman Tyler Manncrossed in 17:10 for a 22-second PR.

Before Mann crossed the line, Wellesley got their entire lineup across with Solomon Jin (17:06), Peter Mahoney (17:07), and Aidan Abazaj (17:08). This pack gave Wellesley the edge and ultimately decided the win.

Keep an eye on both these teams heading into the postseason. Their performances specifically at this meet show us that they have a shot at making it to the All-State meet, even inside the most competitive divisions.

#15 Longmeadow defeats Northampton (18-40)

Course note: Longmeadow's home course is short.

Longmeadow took a big win over Northampton on Tuesday, earning the title of Western Mass champions.

CJ Deliso (16:11) and Jacob Abebe (16:11) led the race from start to finish to go 1-2 in a very controlled effort. Longmeadow’s Finn O’Connor finished right behind the two in 16:14. Henry Daggotthen led the Northampton squad to finish in 16:22. Longmeadow then sealed the win with big performances by Aidan Kretschmar (16:25) andHenry Cournoyer(16:30).

Although this course is short, keep in mind that Daggot ran 16:17 at Wrentham earlier this season at the Frank Kelley Invitational. Using this knowledge we're making the assumption that Longmeadow’s back-end has made some big improvements.

These performances by O’Connor, Kretschmar, and Cournoyer should bring Longmeadow even higher in the rankings to contest for the Division 2 title.

Milton Defeats Braintree (18-43)

The Milton boys are a program that keeps going under our radar but are definitely not a team to be trifled with. As a team that hasn’t even made the All-State meet in the past couple years, this team seems to be turning their program around.

Bobby Beatotook first place in 15:27 on the 2.9 mile-course on Wednesday evening. Although Beato is more known for his mid-distance capabilities, he’s still an amazing front runner for the Milton squad. He also took the school record on his home course beating some key alumni like Robert Carew and Chris Sullivan.

Milton’s Gideon Toth (16:03) and Nate Turner (16:07) were next to cross the line. Both individuals put up great performances to put them #6 and #7 all time for Milton on their home course. Toth in particular had a great day with him usually being a back-end varsity scorer and now beating Turner who has consistently been the teams #2 runner.

Braintree’sKyle Koca was next to cross the line in 16:14. Keep in mind that he boasts a 5K PR of 16:27. Will Deren (16:43) placed 5th and Ryan Ellis (17:07) placed 7th to both round out the Milton squad.

Keep an eye on Milton heading into the postseason as they're not a team to sleep on.

Walpole defeats Weymouth (26-31)

Patrick Geagan separated himself from the rest of the field to take first place in 14:04 on the 2.8 mile course. Although this course isn’t a 5K, it's very comparable with a time in the 15:30 - 15:40 range.

Walpole’s Nate O’Neil took second in 14:39, with teammate Joe Curran following behind in 14:54. Weymouth’s Andrew Himmer then got in the middle of the Walpole pack to run 15:01, while Walpole’s Ethan Hamilton was right behind in 15:04.

Weymouth’s freshmanJames Glavinalso had a great day to place 6th overall in 15:21.

Walpole eventually took the win by placing all 7 runners in front of Weymouth’s #5. Camden Triessl(16:12),Arthava Kulkarni (16:22), and Onyx Evans(16:31) rounded out the Walpole squad.

North Andover goes Undefeated

North Andover ran its final dual meet this week against Andover and Central Catholic to finish 10-0 in the MVC.

Andover’sLandon Zoss won the meet in 15:13 on the 3-mile course. North Andover’s Tucker LaRosa then put on a phenomenal performance to take 2nd in 15:23. To put that into perspective, LaRosa was only 10 seconds behind Zoss and over 20 seconds ahead of 3rd place.

North Andover’s Rand brothers then locked up 3rd and 4th with Liam Rand running 15:45 and Tristan Rand running 15:56. Central Catholic’s Wesley Otero then crossed the line in 5th for 15:57, barely being outkicked by T. Rand.

North Andover then showed off their impressive depth. Cam Ricciocrossed the line in 16:03. Riccio was followed by Carter Eastwick-Haskell (16:04),Owen Desmand (16:06), and Will Thomas (16:09). Thomas is usually one of the squad’s top runners so we are assuming he did not have his best day.

North Andover beat Andover with a score of 20-43 and Central Catholic 17-46. Andover also took the win over Central Catholic 25-32.

Lowell defeats Billerica (20-38)

We wouldn’t really call this an upset as it doesn’t look like Billerica tried to go for the win.

Lowell’s Samuel DeSouza took 1st place in 14:12 on the 2.75 mile course. Billerica’s Sahil Gandhi then followed in 14:20. This looks like it may have been a tempo for Gandhi based on what we’ve seen from him earlier this season. Still that doesn’t mean DeSouza is a bad runner, he is definitely someone to watch going into the postseason.

Three more Lowell runners then crossed the line to secure the win.Elijah Calderon led the squad in 14:24, barely behind Gandhi. Bradey Meade (14:48) and Maylonzo Wilson (15:00) then followed. Billerica freshman Dylan Kane then placed 6th in 15:10.

Mason Nileswas notably absent from this meet. He didn’t have his best performance last week, that though the reason for his absence isn't clear.

North Andover finishes its season 10-0 in the MVC, Lowell ends 9-1, and Billerica ends 8-2.
