Conference Conclusion Part 1: Week in Review

Lexington dominates, Brookline shows its depth, Hopedale stays hot, and BC High pulls off a major upset.

By Ethan Barossi · October 28, 2026

BC High seniors celebrating their upset win over St. John's Prep at the Catholic Conference Championship.

Middlesex League Championship

Note: Although Woburn Country Club is known for being a fast course, we believe it ran slower than a typical 5K this year.

The Middlesex League was the first major league to hold its conference championship, and it put on quite a show.

Spotlight — Lexington

#6 Lexington continued its dominance with an impressive 1-2-3 finish, taking home the team title with 32 points. Arlington finished second with 65.

Patrick Noonan won the meet in 15:36, followed by Trevor Stevens in 15:48. The biggest surprise came from Alden Hall, who finished third in 15:52 after entering with a previous PR of 16:17.

Despite being only two races into his return, Rhys Behlings ran 16:26 for 12th. Benjamin Sbihli followed in 14th at 16:42, while Andrew Zhang finished 19th in 16:57.

With its top runners returning to form, Lexington is looking dangerous heading into November.

Spotlight — Arlington

Arlington didn't appear to have its best day, but still managed to hold off Reading Memorial, 65-69.

Owen Merrill led the squad in eighth at 16:18, followed closely by Eli Hanover (16:20) and Zachary Weinberg (16:21) in ninth and tenth.

The back end struggled more than usual, with Evan Jacobsan placing 17th in 16:49 and Nathan Wilson finishing 21st in 17:02.

Based on previous performances, this likely wasn't Arlington's best showing. Reading's Declan Grant also finished ahead of the entire Arlington lineup, which came as a surprise.

Spotlight — Reading Memorial

After last week's performance, Reading appeared to be getting things together. Unfortunately, its inconsistency showed again.

Max King dropped out for the third time this season. King has looked strong when healthy, but his availability remains a major concern heading into Division 1C.

Finn Johnson-Houlihan also didn't have his best day, finishing 13th in 16:34. He's dealt with injury issues recently, so this may have been more of a rust-buster.

The bright spot was Declan Grant, who continued his impressive stretch with a seventh-place finish in 16:10. The Runner of the Week has been one of Reading's most consistent performers.

Colin Herlihy (16:45.3) placed 15th, followed by Colin Carroll (16:45.4) in 16th. Curran Healy finished 18th in 16:53.

Reading enters November as the top seed in Division 1C, but will need to be healthy to hold off Oliver Ames.

Spotlight — Belmont

Belmont's talented trio swept fourth through sixth place behind Lexington's top three.

Noah Geiger led the way in 15:57, followed by Will Olmsted, who ran a 29-second PR of 15:59. Ishaan Kanagaraj finished right behind in 16:02.

The team's back end struggled, with Paul Son (17:44), Lucas Schuster (17:51), and Elias Campisano (17:58) placing 33rd, 36th, and 38th.

Belmont finished fourth overall, just 10 points behind Reading.

The Middlesex League closes its season with four teams inside the state Top 20. Reading remains the biggest question mark heading into the postseason, especially with Oliver Ames waiting in Division 1C.

HOKA Postal Nationals

Brookline traditionally competes at HOKA Postal Nationals, but this year the meet fell during the same week as its conference championship. That didn't stop the Warriors from showing up with another impressive performance.

Liam Hartmann won the two-mile in a blistering 9:16, while Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem followed with a PR of 9:18. This duo continues to look dangerous heading into the postseason.

Boston Latin Academy's Adam Kramer (9:31.7) narrowly outleaned teammate Bradon Spiess (9:31.7) for third and fourth. Nate Iliff rounded out BLA's trio in sixth at 9:42.

Brookline's back-end talent was the biggest story.

Matt Ritter finished fifth in 9:40, showing that his mid-distance speed can translate to longer distances. Elias Thomasson (9:46), Nam Danh (9:51), Toby Paradise (9:52), and Wei Chandler (9:58) all broke 10 minutes.

Another major surprise came from freshman Kieran Morrissey-Dhenke, who ran an incredible 9:54. After winning freshman races at both Frank Kelley and Twilight, he has now broken 10 minutes for two miles in the middle of cross-country season.

Morrissey-Dhenke could even challenge for a varsity spot this postseason, which says a lot about Brookline's depth.

Brookline's freshman squad currently holds the nation's top reported two-mile team average at 10:36.

The Warriors return Saturday for the Bay State Conference Championship. Cambridge should be watching closely, as Brookline continues to close the gap.

CMAC Championship

Hopedale continued its momentum with another convincing victory over Division 3 rival Parker Charter, winning 28-57.

Quinn Cook took the individual title in 15:39, defeating Parker Charter's Jett Johnson (15:46). With Fort Devens being a tougher course, both performances were impressive.

Hopedale's Ben Stone finished third in 16:13, ahead of Whitinsville Christian's Benjamin Barron (16:25). Stone continues to improve and looks capable of breaking 16 minutes on a faster course.

Connor Fitzgibbon (16:31) and Cedric Arthur (16:34) followed in fifth and sixth. Arthur was one of the biggest surprises, moving from Hopedale's back end talent into its top pack while recording a PR.

Parker Charter's Danny Garrison (16:45) and Nathan Davis (16:59) finished ahead of Hopedale's Jack Murphy (17:17), but Hopedale had already secured the win.

It's worth noting that Parker Charter was missing its third and fourth runners, who have also been absent from recent meets.

Whitinsville Christian also looked promising to finish third, just 10 points behind Parker Charter. Nathaniel Vos (16:52) placed tenth, while Demetri Boermeester (17:22) and Michael Vos (17:26) finished 14th and 15th.

Hopedale continues to look like one of the strongest Division 3 teams heading into November.

Catholic Conference Championship

Tuesday night delivered the biggest upset of the week as #9 BC High defeated #5 St. John's Prep, 44-48.

It was revenge for BC High after Prep upset the Eagles at last year's championship. Considering how both teams had performed this season, few expected this result.

Colin Kurtz won the meet in 15:24 after spending much of the race behind Xaverian's Jack Weintraub (15:28). Kurtz found another gear in the final stretch to secure the victory.

Prep's Leo Seltenrich finished third in 15:44, while Holden Hallee delivered a major surprise with a 34-second PR to place fourth.

BC High's Joseph Hayes ran 15:54.9 for fifth, narrowly beating Prep's Henry McDonnell (15:56).

Then BC High's depth took over.

Braeden Donovan ran a massive 62-second PR of 16:06 for 11th. Theo Weiss, who has dealt with injuries recently, followed in 13th at 16:15.

Freshman Cooper Wilson (16:18) and Nolan Kurtz (16:23) placed 14th and 15th. Wilson, who owns a 9:14 PR for 3000 meters, showed impressive strength over 5K.

St. John's Prep didn't appear to have its best day, recording only two season bests and no PRs. Liam Callahan (16:03), Mateo DeOrio (16:16), and Sam Valentine (16:26) rounded out the team's scoring five.

We would still give Prep the edge based on its overall season, but BC High is right on its heels. These teams will meet again at Division 1A and All-States.

Xaverian

Although BC High stole the spotlight, Xaverian also delivered one of the week's best performances.

Despite injury concerns involving Emile Brodeur and Scott Feeney, Xaverian finished third with 62 points.

After Weintraub's runner-up finish, Marco Petrino ran 15:58.1 for seventh and Alex Armstrong clocked 15:58.6 for ninth. Petrino recorded a 50-second PR, while Armstrong improved by 36 seconds.

Anders Hallman followed in 20th with a 55-second PR of 16:32.

With some of the team's usual scorers unavailable, Luke Herman stepped up as the fifth runner, placing 25th in a PR of 16:57.

Xaverian finished just 14 points behind St. John's Prep and 30 ahead of Catholic Memorial.

Catholic Memorial

Catholic Memorial also put together an encouraging performance after a relatively quiet stretch since its strong opener at the Vineyard Invitational.

Gerard Stock ran 15:58.5 for eighth, splitting Xaverian's Petrino and Armstrong.

Theo Fitzpatrick followed with a nine-second PR for 16th, while Jude Kaleba placed 18th in 16:27. Kaleba hasn't had his best season up until this point, but improved his season best by 55 seconds, a promising sign heading into November.

Finn Rosebaum added another PR, finishing 23rd in 16:43.

Unfortunately though, Catholic Memorial has lost Daniel Kaleba for the remainder of the season due to injury. He was one of the team's strongest performers earlier in the fall.

Even with that setback, Catholic Memorial showed enough on Tuesday to remain a serious contender at Division 1C, just falling in behind Reading, Oliver Ames, and Billerica.
