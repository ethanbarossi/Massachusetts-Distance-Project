Conference Conclusion Part 2: Weekend in Review

By Ethan Barossi · November 2, 2026

The remaining Massachusetts conference championships wrapped up over the weekend, and they certainly didn’t disappoint. Here are some of the biggest moments that defined the weekend across the state.

Dual County League

The Dual County league started off the weekend with a big performance by Cambridge on Friday night.

Cambridge Rindge & Latin placed four runners in the top seven with Leon Ibanez-Fraile taking the individual title in 15:43 on the difficult conditions of the Franklin Park course.

One of the biggest surprises was Cambridge’s No. 2 runner, Gael Berzin. He is usually their No. 5 runner but ultimately took third place on the day to surpass multiple of his teammates. He also notched a PR of 15:53.

Thomas Dewitt (15:59) and Liam Eldridge (16:01) were next to cross the line for Cambridge. Although they placed fifth and sixth overall, these teams also swept the top 4 spots in the Thorpe division of the league.

James Selvitella rounded out the squad for 13th place in 16:23, though it's important to note that Kieran Eichler didn’t race. The Cambridge runner was also notably absent for the Catholic Memorial Invitational earlier in the season so he may be dealing with some type of injury.

Although in a different division of the league, Weston placed second as a team on the day. Hunter Waechter again led this squad with a fourth place finish in 15:55. Though as opposed to the twilight meet, Matthew Fitzgibbon was closely behind placing eighth overall in 16:03. Although dealing with some injury issues early in the season Fitzgibbon seems to be fully back to health, providing Weston with one of the best 1-2 punches in D2.

One of the top freshmen in the state, Jonah Watkins, finished 2third overall in 16:48, followed by Kevin Han in 17:08.

Although one of their top runners in Thomas Moser didn’t have his best day, the return of one of their top runners made up for it. Aarav Mehta finished 3third overall in 17:14 to close out the Weston squad.

Concord-Carlisle finished next to edge out Lincoln-Sudbury on a sixth man tiebreaker. Concord-Carlisle didn’t nearly have their best performance with many individuals underperforming and not showing the skill we saw earlier at the Catholic Memorial Invitational.

Most notably the squad was led by Will Okie (16:24), who's usually in their back-end pack. With Toby Martel (16:25) finishing right behind. Their No. 1 runner in Braeden Quayle most notably was not on his game with him finishing as the squad’s fourth runner in 16:42.

Hopefully this team will be able to rebound as their Divisional rival in North Andover had a great showing at the conference meet, but we’ll touch on that later.

Lincoln-Sudbury performed well mainly because of its top runners. Joshua Gamel had a day finishing seventh overall in 16:02 with Brandon Ma (16:21) and Lukas Masiakos (16:24) coming in next for the squad.

Newton South finished farther back than expected; however, they were out one of their top runners, and Carl Munshi did not seem to have his best day.

Charlie Guthrie put on one of the most interesting performances of the meet, with the Boston Latin runner placing second only behind Ibanez-Fraile. He wasn’t necessarily on our radar, but he just put up one of the best performances of the year to put him as a top contender inside Division 1.

Bay State Conference

The biggest individual upset came at the Bay State Conference Championship where Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem took home the title in a PR of 15:23. Going into the race it was our assumption that the main battle would be between Brookline’s Liam Hartmann and Newton North’s Ryan Costello, with Abdel-Dayem competing for third with Weymouth’s Patrick Geagan.

Abdel-Dayem sat on his teammate for the first half of the race and then took control, now making him the No. 2 seed for the D1A race. Hartmann placed second overall in 15:25, followed by Costello in 15:29, then Geagan in 15:31. 

Some other notable individuals include Needham’s Connor Bobbin placing seventh in 15:58, Newton North’s Alex Daly finishing right behind in 16:00, Brookline freshman Kieran Morrissey-Dhenke placing 12th in 16:11, Braintree’s Kyle Koca following right behind in 16:13, and Framingham’s James Feaster finishing 1sixth in 16:19.

Brookline captured another Bay State Conference title, defeating Newton North 31–63. Abdel-Dayem and Hartmann went 1-2 followed by six more Warriors making the podium. 

Elias Thomasson led the second overall pack of runners to place sixth in 15:58, Matt Ritter placed tenth in 16:09, Morrissey-Dhenke came in next followed by Nam Danh (16:21) in 1seventh, while Toby Paradise (16:31) and Wei Chandler (16:32) rounded out the squad in 2first and 2second.

Although Newton North only placed second they still put up a really good fight. Will Keneally was the squad’s No. 3 finisher in 16:15 for 1fifth place overall. We’ve already discussed this but he seems to be getting back into form pretty nicely. After a slow start to his season he has taken a big jump in their depth chart.

Colin Van Vliet led the next group in 1eighth place overall, followed by Eddie Howell and Jamie Evans all in the 16:30 range. Lucas Allen is still notably a bit farther back finishing 30th overall in 16:47. If this group wants a chance to move up the rankings Allen needs to be at full strength.

Natick was third overall coming in with 85 points. Declan Hava (15:37) led the squad in fifth followed by Benjamin Gidelson (16:04) in ninth. 

Callum Doyle was the next Redhawk to cross the line in 16:14 for 1fourth place overall, though it doesn’t seem like he is having his best season. Alex Forte (16:43), Kai Vickers-Richard (16:47), and Sasha Rennie (16:47) rounded out the squad.

Framingham then placed fourth for one of the biggest surprises of the week. They're definitely a team that we haven’t talked about enough but we didn’t think that they were at the same level as teams like Needham and Wellesley. Framingham scored 143 points followed by Needham in 146 then Wellesley in 153.

This team got our attention a few weeks ago with them edging out Catholic Memorial and taking home a trophy at the CMI, though we did sleep on them as we didn’t think Catholic Memorial had their best day. After that they actually took down rival team Natick on their senior night, though Natick was resting multiple runners.

Now they’ve proven they can actually compete with some top-tier teams. Bennett Dalton (16:33) had a big PR to finish second for the squad in 2third, just making the podium. Nathan Marden (16:48), Evan Baldwin-Juhas (17:01) and Dylan Wheeler (17:05) rounded out the squad.

Though notably Needham did get some revenge on Wellesley to edge them out by 7 points. The squad was highlighted by Bobbin and Cash Lindsley (16:42) outleaning Wellesley's Daniel Goldberg (16:42) at the line.

Hockomock League

Oliver Ames once again conquered the league with no other team really up in their area. 

Corey Yifru (15:55) and Devon McStowe (15:57) led the squad to place second and third overall. It appears McStowe was trying to have a bit of a better race at the start though fell off with Yifru getting him in the final lap. We know McStowe is still coming off a sickness so we think he can run a lot faster. 

Peter Feordoroff (16:19) came in next for the squad to place seventh overall, with Jake Hauvey (16:43) falling off a bit to place 1fourth overall. We know Hauvey is capable of being in the 16:20 range so be on the lookout for him. Nico Colvin then rounded out the squad in 1ninth place with a PR of 16:55.

Be on the lookout for Oliver Ames in the postseason as they are getting closer to Reading with every single performance.

Sharon then came in to upset Mansfield 89–93. Both squads put up a good fight, though Sharon just had a better day.

Nikita Cherapukha led the Mansfield squad in 16:02 for fifth place overall while Walter Stock (16:37) followed behind in 12th. Both runners didn’t PR so that was one of the big contributions to Sharon's victory over the squad. 

Freshman Barrett Reynolds (16:49) came in next for the squad in 1sixth place, followed by Bobby Stock (17:20) in 2seventh and Everett Foster (17:33) in 3third place.

For Sharon, they clearly wanted this big second place taking home 4 PRs and a season best. 

Alex Pinnix (16:19) led the squad in sixth overall finishing just behind Cherapukha. Kavish Raja (16:25) and Matteo Lozano (16:53) also had big races to both walk away with big PRs.

Though Riley Newbold was probably the biggest reason for the team's win as he clocked a 23-second PR to place 2first overall in 16:58. Jack Foster rounded out this squad in 3sixth overall for 17:39.

On the individual side North Attleborough's Anthony Malakidis clocked the win in 15:17. Be sure to keep your eye on this runner in the postseason as he has been known to perform. Malakidis is the No. 2 returner from last year's Meet of Champions and can clearly compete with top individuals like Liam Hartmann and the Billerica duo.

Merrimack Valley League

The MVC Championship delivered plenty of highlights on both the individual and team sides.

Billerica’s Mason Niles (15:27) got revenge for his earlier dual-meet loss as he took first place overall barely getting Andover’s Landon Zoss (15:28) at the line. This is a big win for Niles as we're pretty sure he was dealing with a minor injury in weeks prior.

Sahil Gandhi (15:35) then had an even closer matchup where he just edged Lowell’s Samuel De Souza (15:35) at the line. For Gandhi this is a bit of an underwhelming performance as he’s still current MA #1, though De Souza had a great race to put him in podium contention at the D1A meet in the coming weeks. 

Dracut’s Mogavero brothers then took fifth and eighth overall with senior Tyler running 15:49 and freshman Ryan running 16:03

Though this is where North Andover’s depth took over. As expected from his performance the past couple weeks, Tucker LaRosa (15:58) led the squad in sixth overall, followed immediately by Liam Rand (15:59) in seventh.

Though the biggest performance of the team was actually from Tristan Rand as he was the only member of the top squad to grab a PR. Rand finished ninth overall with a PR of 16:14. Owen Desmand (16:21) then came in 11th while Cameron Riccio (16:27) rounded out the squad in 1fifth. Will Thomas, the squad’s No. 1 runner, was notably absent.

North Andover took the team title with 48 points, the team’s first conference win since joining the conference in 2012. Though there was an extremely tight team battle behind them. Andover upset Billerica to take second overall 89–94 with Lowell followed extremely close behind with 96 points.

Andover upset the team by placing all 5 runners below 17:00. Elye Berman (16:31) finished second on the team besides Zoss. Alex Hoffenberg (16:46), Jack Matson (16:46), and Ryan Batts (16:54) rounded out the squad. 

Though in Billerica’s case, it's important to note that they were out one of their top runners in Dylan Kane. Kane probably would’ve been able to run in the 16:30 range as he’s usually up with teammate Max Gorski who ran 16:33 on the day. Hayden Headley (17:19) and Shaunak Kulkarni (17:25) rounded out the squad.

Some other notable performances came from Central Catholic’s Wesley Otero, who ran 16:17 for tenth place overall to lead his team to a fifth place finish, and Elijah Calderon, who was Lowell’s No. 2 runner placing 12th overall in 16:24.

It’s uncommon to hold top runners out of conference championships, so we wouldn’t be surprised if Kane or Thomas were dealing with some injuries. They will definitely both be needed at the divisional meets as both teams are in contention for team trophies. Let’s hope both athletes are healthy.

Patriot League

As far as team upsets go, Pembroke may have pulled off the biggest one of the year. Although they don’t get covered a lot and are in one of the smaller leagues in the state, Pembroke found a way to break out and take gold in the Patriot league. Keep in mind that when we made our predictions for who would win this league in September, Pembroke wasn’t even inside the top 3.

They beat out Marshfield 66–75 with the team Overwhelming Marshfield’s top-end. Nate Palmieri took first in 15:58 followed immediately by teammate Logan Holloway in 16:02. Holloway’s win is even more impressive when you consider that he beat rival Conor Rightmire (16:07) from Marshfield. 

Rightmire finished third overall, followed by two more teammates in fifth and sixth: Archie Meberg (16:21) and Cal Kennedy (16:23).

After that, Sam Ruggiero had an amazing race and one of the biggest keys to Pembroke’s win. Ruggiero finished 12th overall in 16:39, a 28-second PR for the runner. 

Pembroke’s No. 4 and No. 5 then edged out both Marshfield runners with Pembroke’s Marco Gulinelo (17:09) placing 2first and Daniel Finn (17:38) placing 30th. Marshfield was rounded out by Conor Keough (17:32) and Emmett Aufiero (17:55) for 2seventh and 3fourth respectively.

Plymouth South finished third, just behind the two teams with 86 points. They had a very tight spread of 18 seconds, with only 2 other runners being in the middle of the squad. Xavier Jocham (16:49) led the squad in 1fourth followed by Jake Parks (16:52) for 1sixth, CJ Fox (16:55) for 1seventh, Joseph Dimartinis (17:01) for 1ninth, and Kenji Gallo (17:07) for 20th.

Behind them, there was even more drama. Silver Lake barely edged out Plymouth North on a sixth man tiebreaker, both teams scored 100 points. 

Plymouth North had some serious top-end talent in Dylan Vaughn (16:16) placing fourth overall, Aidan Humberd (16:33) placing eighth, and Jack Tobin (16:37) placing 11th. However, they lacked key depth which resulted in their loss. Their No. 4 and No. 5 came through in 3fifth and 4second.

Silver Lake was led by Graham Laws (16:36) placing tenth overall, and Owen Hughes (16:40) placing 12th. Though they had less top-end strength, they made up for it in their depth. Their No. 3 - No. 5 placed 2fourth, 2fifth, and 2eighth respectively, giving Silver Lake the win. A special shoutout goes to Andrew DeBlois who won the sixth man tiebreaker as he placed 2ninth overall.
