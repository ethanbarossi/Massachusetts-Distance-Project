Ocean State Invitational: What to Expect

Massachusetts' best collide at Ocean State as top teams, Division 2 contenders, and some of the state's fastest individuals prepare for a major early-season test.

By Ethan Barossi · September 22, 2026
Walpole’s Luke Zahurak (middle) running at the 2023 Ocean State Invitational. Photo: Kenny Forester.

The Ocean State Invite is shaping up to be the biggest invitational of the year. With 17 of the top 20 teams in the state set to make an appearance, the invite can be seen as a preview for the All-State meet in two months.

During this invitational, we’ll get to see the top seven teams in the state go head-to-head, along with the top five teams in Division 2. After this meet, we’ll get a real sense of where teams are heading into October and the work that they would need to do to come out on top.

Championship Outlook

We still think that, as of right now, Cambridge Rindge & Latin is the top team in the state and should be taking home 1st here. We didn’t originally realize this; however, they have another returner at 16:20 who was injured last cross country season. James Selvitella should be able to solve their depth problem and help them take away 1st place.

However, there are a couple of teams right behind them that we think have the chance to pull off the upset.

#2 Lexington will be present with their entire squad. They opened their season last weekend at the Manchester Invitational with a breakout performance by Trevor Stevens. Let’s see if Stevens can keep his momentum going into this weekend.

#5 Concord-Carlisle has their first real opener this weekend. They were going to announce themselves at the Amherst Invitational but made the decision to scratch the race. Last year around this time, we saw a breakout performance by the team at the Bay State Invitational. Let’s see if they can prove themselves once again.

#3 Brookline is also entering their entire squad. We don’t feel as though this team is ready to challenge some of these teams just yet. Although the times from their 2.76-mile course may be a bit hard to figure out, we feel they’re currently a bit farther back from some of these top teams and won’t really be able to show us something until the Twilight Invitational in mid-October. Still, the Warriors always have a way of surprising us.

#6 Newton North also seems like they’re a bit farther back as well. They’re still getting their entire lineup together, with their #2 returner, Lucas Allen, set to have his first race this weekend. Still, we expect Ryan Costello to be up at the top of the race, with him being the favorite from the state of Massachusetts.

#4 BC High will most likely not be in contention due to Owen Geagan not having been entered in the race.

Though don’t sleep on #7 St. John’s Prep. They were in a similar position around this time last year and came up to win this meet, beating #2 Natick and #3 Lexington in the rankings at the time.

As opposed to last weekend, #10 North Andover enters this race with their entire squad. They had a big victory last weekend at the Amherst Invitational, but they were without their entire varsity lineup. This weekend, Tristan Rand and Will Thomas look to be back and move their team even higher in the rankings.

Division 2 Outlook

The top five teams in Division 2 are all entered in this meet: Longmeadow, Boston Latin Academy, Groton-Dunstable, Weston, and Walpole.

Of these teams, Walpole is the only one not entered in the championship race, though we feel they are a bit behind the other four teams as they don’t have any top-end talent at the front of the race.

Originally, we thought that Weston was the #3 seed in Division 2; however, we still have yet to see Aarav Mehta. If he isn’t healthy, their next returner doesn’t come in until 17:51. Losing a sub-16:00 runner is a big loss for a small team and, for now, puts them at the #4 seed.

It’s hard to say where the Longmeadow and Boston Latin Academy teams will be this weekend. Though Longmeadow probably wants some revenge after their matchup last year at the Division 2 All-State meet. Judging by the entries, it seems BLA hasn’t found another runner to enter their #5 position. Mason Mastrodicasa still holds that spot with a PR of 18:19. So this mainly depends on whether BLA’s top end can counter their back end.

For Longmeadow, having Jacob Abebe at full strength would be a big edge for the team. Two weeks ago, he started his race out with CJ Deliso but fell off by around a minute. Still, we’re very interested to see how these two teams battle at this meet, at Division 2A, and inside Division 2.

Finally, we feel that Groton-Dunstable is the team to beat. Their 1-2 punch of Andrew Kosiba and Greyson Duane currently can’t be matched by any other team in the division. They also have lots of depth and a family dynasty of two more Duane brothers to back them up. With their entire squad entered, we’ll get a great look at where they stand going into October.

Individual Outlook

Although we are uncertain about individuals from other states, the clear favorite seems to be Newton North’s Ryan Costello. He’s only one of three runners who have broken the 16:00 barrier so far this season, and he’s already done it twice. Although Ocean State has a bit of harder terrain, he’s one of the few who might be able to get under 16 minutes.

Although they haven’t run a real opener yet, we presume either one or both members of the Billerica duo will be right up there as well. Mason Niles and Sahil Gandhi have tempoed the past few meets and will most likely be going all out for the first time this weekend.

We’ve already talked about Trevor Stevens, but when you really put some time into his performance from last weekend, we can easily assume that he’s very capable of being under 16 minutes as well. We presume he’ll be right up there with the top pack.

Some other key runners include North Attleboro’s Anthony Malakidis, currently MA #2 in the 5K; Concord-Carlisle’s Jackson Misek, who opens his XC season this weekend after being the #1 overall returner from both the indoor and outdoor All-State two miles; and finally Brookline’s Liam Hartmann, the #1 overall returner from the Meet of Champions last fall.
