Week 3 Power Rankings

St. John’s Prep makes a massive jump, Arlington continues to climb, and Xaverian enters the rankings for the first time.

By Ethan Barossi · September 27, 2026
St. John’s Prep huddling before their dual meet with BC High.

Three weeks into the season, we're starting to get a much better idea of which teams are legitimate contenders and which teams still have questions to answer. This week brought some of the biggest movement we've had yet, with St. John's Prep making a massive jump, Arlington continuing to climb, and Xaverian entering the rankings for the first time.

At the same time, injuries and missing runners have started to change the outlook for several of our preseason favorites. Here's where our Top 20 stands heading into Week 4.

Brookline Gains Another Spot

Brookline slides up another spot to #2 this week. This move is mainly the result of other teams dropping in the rankings, but the Warriors are continuing to get closer to where they want to be.

Over the past few weeks, Liam Hartmann and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem have proven to be a powerful 1–2 punch at the front, and there's a strong pack forming behind them. Matt Ritter, Nam Danh, Elias Thomasson, Noah Karp, and Toby Paradise all finished their latest race within 15 seconds of each other.

That type of depth is exactly what Brookline will need later in the season. Watch out for the Warriors this weekend at the Bay State Invitational, where we could finally get to see what this team can do during its first all-out 5K.

We Should’ve Never Doubted St. John’s Prep

After their first couple of races, we started to question what the boys from Prep would be able to put together this season. Their performance this week completely changed our expectations.

St. John's Prep dominated #4 BC High, 16–44, while finishing with a ridiculous 10-second spread from its first through fifth runners. They slide all the way up to #3.

Leo Sentinrich led the squad this week after being absent from Prep's matchup against Xaverian, which was one of the reasons we had started to doubt this team. Mateo DeOrio, Liam Callahan, Henry McDonnell, and Sam Valentine followed right behind him, with only one BC High runner breaking up the Prep pack.

Their five scorers finished between 16:37 and 16:47, but this appears to be a slower course. We believe that on faster terrain like Wrentham, this pack could be capable of running somewhere around the 16:20–16:30 range.

That makes a potential matchup with Concord-Carlisle extremely interesting. The two teams are built in very similar ways, with strong packs and very little separation between their scorers. We currently give Prep the slight advantage, especially if Concord-Carlisle continues to be without Jackson Misek.

BC High, Lexington, and Concord-Carlisle Are Missing Key Runners

At the beginning of the season, we said there could be seven teams in the hunt for the state title. Three weeks later, Lexington and BC High currently look a little farther back than we expected.

That isn't necessarily because they haven't lived up to their potential. In fact, it's almost the opposite. Neither team has been able to put its complete lineup on the course.

BC High's #2 returner, Owen Geagan, hasn't raced yet this season. Lexington has also been without several major returners, including Eric House, Andrew Zhang, and Rhys Behling. If those runners make it back into their respective lineups, both teams still have the potential to climb right back toward the top. For now, though, their absences have to affect where we rank them.

Concord-Carlisle is in a slightly different situation. The Boston Globe reported that the team is currently missing one of its top runners, and based on the lineup we've seen, we believe that runner may be Jackson Misek.

For some context, Misek is the top returning two-miler from both the indoor and outdoor Meet of Champions. Losing a runner of that caliber would be a massive hit to almost any team in Massachusetts.

Concord-Carlisle, however, has enough depth to absorb some of that loss. That's why St. John's Prep is the only team we're moving ahead of them this week. If Misek eventually returns, Concord-Carlisle immediately becomes an even bigger threat at the top of the state.

Arlington Keeps Getting Deeper

We were already surprised when Arlington entered our rankings last week. Somehow, they've given us another reason to move them up.

The Spy Ponders climb another two spots to #11, largely because they've added another varsity-level runner to an already talented lineup: Roberto Marrocchelli-Mendez.

The freshman made his varsity debut with a 16:41 5K and immediately gives Arlington another major piece. Their previous #5 sat at 17:16. Now, Arlington has five runners between 16:07 and 16:41.

That's a huge difference.

The team's victory over Lexington only makes the argument stronger. Arlington isn't just showing potential anymore; they're starting to produce results against some of the best teams in Massachusetts.

Division 1B was already crowded, and Arlington has now put itself right behind teams like Natick and North Andover. What once looked like a battle between a few clear favorites is quickly turning into one of the deepest divisions in the state.

Xaverian Is No Longer a Sleeper

Another Division 1B team. Who would've thought?

Xaverian enters our rankings all the way up at #13 following an impressive victory over Catholic Memorial. Looking back, we probably should've had them on the list earlier. We originally viewed St. John's Prep's close matchup with Xaverian as more of a controlled effort from Prep, but after what we've seen since, it appears that the course was simply slower than we initially realized.

Now, with Jack Weintraub back in the lineup, Xaverian looks like a completely different team.

Weintraub appears capable of getting around the 16-minute range for 5K, while Marco Petrino and Alex Armstrong have shown the ability to be around the mid-16s. Behind them, Xaverian has enough depth that its entire varsity group looks capable of challenging the 17-minute barrier.

That's a strong lineup, even within the Catholic Conference.

Their next matchup against BC High could tell us even more. If BC High still doesn't have its complete lineup together, Xaverian has a legitimate chance to pull off another upset.

More importantly, Xaverian has thrown itself directly into the Division 1B conversation. Natick, North Andover, Arlington, and now Xaverian are all beginning to look extremely close.

Belmont Destroys Its Competition

Belmont barely misses our Top 20 this week, but its latest performance was too impressive to ignore.

Noah Geiger (16:03) and Ishaan Kanagaraj (16:04) went 1–2 against Winchester, giving Belmont some serious strength at the front. They were followed by Will Olmsted (16:52) and Elias Campisano (16:53), who finished third and fourth overall.

That gives Belmont four runners under 17 minutes.

The biggest question remains the #5 position. If Belmont can find another runner capable of closing the gap to Olmsted and Campisano, this team has a very good chance of breaking into the Top 20.

Three weeks into the season, these rankings already look significantly different from what we expected during the preseason. St. John's Prep has reestablished itself as a serious title contender, Arlington and Xaverian have completely changed the outlook of Division 1B, and several of the state's preseason favorites are still waiting to get their full lineups together.

October is where things really start to matter. With major invitationals and championship meets approaching, there's still plenty of time for this Top 20 to change.
