Picture from masslive.com Title: Massachusetts Top 15 Preseason Rankings (15 - 11) With the duel meet season starting up in just a couple of days, we’ve decided to do a preseason ranking on the top 15 teams that will have the most success this fall. This ranking was made with a combination of athletic.net data, knowledge of cross country courses, and knowledge of the teams themselves. Honorable Mentions - Arlington and Amherst Pelham Both these teams barely missed making this list but we still thought they were worth being talked about. Although both strong teams, Amherst does edge out Arlington by a bit. Arlington’s times seem to be deceiving due to the easy conditions of the Woburn country club at the M12 league meet. On paper it looks like their teams range 5k times from 15:38 - 16:40, however it's safe to assume the M12 league meet wasn’t a real 5k and it's therefore reasonable to add 30 - 45 seconds to every runner's time. The Arlington team is led by senior Owen Merrill and has a strong pack of its 2 - 4 runners within 20 seconds of each other. Just ahead of them is Amherst-Pelham. They enter the season as the no. 4 team in Division 2, and no. 2 in Division 2B. They are led by front Runner Calvin Miller, who if all goes right, should be in the running for individual state champion as well. Right behind him is Peter Nedeau coming into the season with a 5k PR of 16:07. If he could catch up to Miller, Amherst could be looking at one of the scariest 1-2 punches in division 2. Amherst’s 3-4 runners both enters with PR’s below the 17 minute barrier, but their main problem is with their #5 runner. On paper, incoming sophomore Jeremiah Hayslip enters with a PR of 17:56. If Amherst hopes to challenge the rest of Division 2, their #5 will have to get up and challenge their 3 and 4 runners. #15 - Catholic Memorial Our first team in the top 15 is Catholic Memorial. They return a deep team with a lot of up front talent. Leading the pack is Daniel Kaleba (16:04) with three more strong runners right behind him, Gerard Stock (16:17), Jude Kaleba (16:32), and Theo Fitzpatrick (16:33). Though just like Amherst-Pelham, their main problem lies with their #5. On paper, Finn Rosenbaum enters the season with a PR of 17:58, if he can move up over the course of the season, the team could set themselves up for a chance to take home a trophy at the D1C meet in November. #14 - Weston Last year’s Division 2C champions are in the hunt to reclaim their title and may even be looking farther than that. Weston returns two runners below the 16 minute barrier in Mathew Fitzgibbon and Aarav Mehta. Both runners should be able to work off each other in every race to be a solid 1-2 punch. Hunter Waechter enters as their #3 with a PR of 16:14, and with good training might be able to join the pack with his 2 teammates. To round out this team, Weston has 2 more returners under the 17:30 barrier. Division 2C better watch out for the talent that Weston is bringing back in 2026. #13 - Parker Charter This year Parker Charter hopes to continue its dominance over all of division 3. Last year they placed four runners in the top 20 with Nathaniel Henshaw being the individual Division 3 champion. Although Henshaw graduated, they still return three of those runners that finished in the top 20. The team appears to be led by incoming sophomore Jett Johnson. Last year Johnson was one of the top freshmen in the state, let's see if he can continue his momentum. The team then returns 3 more runners under 17 Cypress Lance (16:14), Danny Garrison (16:20), and Zephyr Schermerhorn (16:44) will all contribute heavily to the team as Division 3 is a lot less stacked than Division 1 or 2 this year. #12 - Longmeadow This pick may surprise some but we believe that Longmeadow has the capabilities to take back their division 2 crown in a meet that could be just as close as last year. On paper it looks like the squad is led by incoming junior CJ Deliso, but in actuality they will more likely be led by Jacob Abebe. Abebe suffered an injury last fall that took a toll on his entire sophomore campaign, though looking at his spring season it appears he’ll be back in full shape this fall. For the first the half of last fall he was their #1 as well, beating teammate Ethan Halpern who went on to be the individual D2A champion. After their front two, they return three more strong runners in Aidan Kretschmar (16:37), Finn O’Connor (16:51), and Ryan Katsafanas (17:07). Don’t sleep on Longmeadow as they are the main sleeper pick for Division 2. Their the #1 seed for Division 2A and the #2 Division 2 team on this list. #11 - Oliver Ames Even after losing main man Landon Sarney, Oliver Ames will still pack a punch going into the 2026 season. They are now led by senior Devon McStowe, last fall he was the main reason for their upset victory at the D1C meet over Reading Memorial because of his crucial 6th place finish, moving up many spots in the last lap of the Wrentham course. Corey Yifru may also be at the top of the pack, he was only a freshman last year but had many amazing accomplishments like a 16:39 5k, and a 9:44 two-mile. There is a good chance we see Yifru running up with McStowe this fall. On paper it looks their #5 runner looks misleading, although he comes in with a PR of 17:41, their #6 runner, Peter Feodoroff, ran a PR of 10:07 for two miles this past spring. If this team finds their spark they may be able to top the Hockomock league, and upset Reading for the second year in a row. Picture from miaa.net Title: Massachusetts Top 15 Preseason Rankings (10th - 1st) With the duel meet season starting up in just a couple of days, we’ve decided to do a preseason ranking on the top 15 teams that will have the most success this fall. This ranking was made with a combination of athletic.net data, knowledge of cross country courses, and knowledge of the teams themselves. #10 - Groton-Dunstable The final Division two team on this list is Groton. With the #1 returner from last year's meet in Andrew Kosiba, as well as a family dynasty of Greyson Duane, Ashton Duane, and Cameron Duane, Groton has all the pieces they need to take it all. This team has been at the top of division two for the past few years now but never actually won the meet. They look to not only look for the Division two win, but hope to win 2B in a landslide. Another thing to note is that Greyson Duane has the potential to be just as fast as Kosiba if he's on his game. If they both work off each other, they will be the best 1-2 punch that division two has to offer. Micah Pierantozzi (16:28), and Ashvik Yadav (16:44) also return as key players for the team. Division two should be just as much of a battle as it was last year and it’ll take a lot for one team to come out on top. #9 - Natick Last year’s state runner ups enter the season with less top end talent but a lot more bodies to fill their depth chart. Although losing their top two runners in John Bianchi and Freddie Van Der Velden, Natick still returns their next nine runners. This year their led by Callum Doyle and Declan Hava, Doyle being the #5 returner from the meet of champs and Hava being #11. Last year Benjamin Gidelson stepped into a major role to be their #5 runner, this year the Natick senior will have an even bigger challenge of trying to stay with Doyle and Hava. Their next three runners are all incoming juniors Dylan Cooke (16:48), Kai Vickers-Richard (16:51), and Brady Collins (16:58). Of these three, the focus may be most on Collins as he popped in the winter to run a two-mile PR of 10:03. Though be sure to keep eyes on Johnathan Pereira, Sasha Rennie, and Connor Chviruk as all three runners had really good spring track seasons. #8 - Reading Memorial It's always sad to hear that your #1 runner has to pack it up due to injury. Last year this happened to Reading which caused some disappointing performances for the team in the post season. This fall Finn Johnson-Houlihan is back and the rockets are ready to reclaim their D1C title. Johnson-Houlihan popped in the spring running 9:25 and 4:23, easily being one of the state's top returners. He's backed up by seniors Andrew Princic and Max King, the #6 and #7 returners from the D1C race. Colin Herlihy and Declan Grant round out the rockets this year. Both had amazing track seasons where Herlihy ran 10:13 for two miles, and Grant ran 4:39 for one mile. There's no doubt that the Reading boys have their eyes set on 1C and were excited to see what they can accomplish. #7 - Newton North This team is such a sleeper that we wrote a whole article about them. Ryan Costello returns for his senior year to battle the best the state has to offer. He's the state's top returner in the two-mile and we’ll easily take down his 16:52 PR. Lucas Allen is right behind him returning with a mile PR of 4:29 from last winter. After that William Kenneally, Alex Daly, and Mathew Gallo return after all running in the 10:00 range for the two-mile this track season. If any team is going to come from behind to win it all we believe this will be them. #6 - BC High It seems that even without Greg McGrath, the BC High team still has big dreams. They look a bit low on paper due to an injury last season with Owen Geagan, however he will be fully recovered going into the fall and ready to challenge for the #1 spot on the team. Geagan will challenge Colin Kurtz for this role. This spring Kurtz ran some standout times of 4:28 and 9:24, while Geagan ran times of 4:23 and 1:56. This duo will be wrecking havoc across the catholic conference. Though don’t sleep on Joseph Hayes, BC High has another returner already under 16 minutes for the 5k, he also placed 8th overall in the mile at the indoor meet of champions boasting a PR of 4:24. Theo Weiss and Logan Barry round out this BC High team, if Barry can connect up to Weiss and Hayes the team will be in a good spot to challenge their catholic conference rival. #5 - Concord Carlisle Although losing some top tier talent, Concord is still one of the top teams in the state and still the #1 seed in the D1B race. They’ll be led by Jackson Misek, who last spring battled the heat to be the #1 returner for the outdoor All-State two-mile. Though Misek probably won’t always be leading this Concord pack as there are four more runners right behind him. Braeden Quayle (16:00), Cameron Smack (16:23), Sawyer Bout (16:24), and Ryan Perry (16:26) all return to the Concord team this year. This team definitely has the potential to win it all in November but they need to correct their mistakes from last year to do so. #4 - St. Johns Prep The Division 1 track champions hope to continue their momentum into the fall with the opportunity to get their third trophy in a row. Their #6 runner on paper might actually be their #1 runner in a situation similar to Costello. Mateo DeOrio might take charge of the team after running some crazy track PR’s of 4:23 and 9:38. Though he won’t be alone up front, Henry McDonnell and Leo Seltenrich both boast PR’s of 15:47 and 15:48 and should show some top end talent for the team. Liam Callahan (16:05), Sam Valentine (16:23), and Hunter Opal (16:28) all round out the teams top 6 runners. This team should have some good battles with BC High inside the catholic conference and challenge the other top teams in the state. #3 - Brookline Do we think that Brookline has one of the best shots to win in November? Yes, however, this is still September. Currently we have them at #3 but knowing Coach Glennon, this team now and this team in 2 months will not be the same. Liam Hartmann and Ibrahim Abdel-Dayem return to both try and score some low points for the warriors. After that they have a pack of five juniors all trying to contribute to what could be their 5th title in a row. Nam Danh (16:30), Elias Thomasson (16:37), Mathew Ritter (16:38), Noah Karp (16:46), and Toby Paradise (16:52) all had massive track seasons to try and contribute to what could be their next trophy. Will the warriors go five in a row? #2 - Lexington For now we won’t focus on their 5k times due to the easy conditions of the M12 course, so we’ll turn to their track times. Patrick Noonan and Trevor Stevens return to challenge the Brookline duo in scoring some low points. Both had great seasons in the two-mile where Noonan ran 9:19 and Stevens ran 9:22 to finish right behind Abdel-Dayem at the outdoor division 1 meet. Aidan Hall (4:26), Owen Ross (4:31), and Andrew Zhang (9:58) all round out the teams top 5.
